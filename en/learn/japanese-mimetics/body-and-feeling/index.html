Body and feeling – Japanese mimetics

Warmth, hunger, nausea and itching – Japanese words for what the body feels, with examples and translations.

  • ぺこぺこ · to be hungry
  • ぽかぽか · to be pleasantly warm
  • むかむか · to feel nauseous
  • むずむず · to itch
  • かっか · to burn with heat
  • ぬくぬく · to be snug and warm
  • ぶよぶよ · to be flabby and soft

ぺこぺこ · to be hungry

An empty stomach — the everyday word for hunger.

Half-voiced ぺ is light and a little pitiful. It speaks of emptiness, not pain.

Examples

  • 朝あさから何なにも食たべていないので、お腹なかがぺこぺこだ。I have not eaten a thing since morning and my stomach is completely empty.
  • お腹なかがぺこぺこの状態じょうたいで走はしると、力ちからが出でない。Running on an empty stomach, you have no strength.

ぽかぽか · to be pleasantly warm

Warmth spreading through the whole body: spring sun, a hot spring, walking yourself warm.

The 清音／半濁音 pair is real here: ほかほか and ぽかぽか. Voiceless ほ keeps the warmth attached to something freshly heated — rice straight from the pot, a bun out of the steamer, someone straight out of the bath. The 半濁点 in ぽ cuts it loose from any source: the warmth arises by itself, from the sun and the weather, with nothing wrapped around you — Japanese says ぽかぽか陽気 for a mild sunny day, never ほかほか陽気. Warmth you have to wrap yourself in is ぬくぬく.

Examples

  • 春はるの日差ひざしで、体からだが芯しんからぽかぽかする。The spring sunshine leaves me pleasantly warm right through.
  • 風かぜのない小春日和こはるびよりで、日ひなたに座すわっているだけで体からだじゅうがぽかぽかだ。A windless, mild late-autumn day: just sitting in the sun leaves my whole body pleasantly warm.

むかむか · to feel nauseous

Your stomach rises; also: simmering anger.

む carries no 濁点 and still sounds muffled and heavy — the nausea wells up inside, without sharp pain. The nasal m is itself voiced: 清音 means no diacritic, not no voice.

Examples

  • 船ふねに酔よって、吐はき気けで胃いがむかむかする。I got seasick and my stomach is churning.
  • 脂あぶらっこい物ものを食たべすぎて、吐はき気けが胃いの底そこからむかむかとこみ上あげる。I ate too much greasy food and nausea keeps rising from the pit of my stomach.

むずむず · to itch

An itch that will not let you sit still.

Voiced ず adds persistence: it does not itch once, it keeps itching. An itch, not a pain: ちくちく pricks and hurts, むずむず only itches and will not let you settle. The same restlessness on the pleasant side is うずうず — nothing itches there, you simply cannot wait to get on with something.

Examples

  • 花粉症かふんしょうで、痒かゆくて我慢がまんできないほど鼻はながむずむずする。My hay fever makes my nose itch past all bearing.
  • 虫むしに刺さされた所ところが痒かゆくて、かきむしりたくなるほどむずむずとして落おち着つかない。The insect bite itches so badly I want to scratch it raw; I cannot sit still.

かっか · to burn with heat

A face or head on fire: shame, anger, fever.

The small っ inside gives a burst of heat that the repeated ぽかぽか lacks — there the warmth is mild and pleasant.

Examples

  • 恥はずかしさで耳みみまで赤あかくなり、顔かおが焼やけるように熱あつくなってかっかする。Shame turned me red to the ears and my face is burning hot.
  • 言いい返かえせなかった悔くやしさで顔かおのほてりが引ひかず、焼やけるように熱あつく、まだかっかしている。The frustration of having had no answer keeps the flush on my face; it is still burning hot.

ぬくぬく · to be snug and warm

Warmth that wraps around you: a quilt, a thick coat.

Enveloping warmth rather than radiating warmth. ぽかぽか arises by itself, from the sun and the weather, with nothing wrapped around you (ぽかぽか陽気). ほかほか is the warmth of something freshly heated: rice straight from the pot, a bun out of the steamer. ぬくぬく is warmth you have to wrap yourself in, and it speaks of the person who is snug in it.

Examples

  • 湯ゆたんぽを入いれた柔やわらかい布団ふとんにくるまって、外そとの寒さむさも知しらずに朝あさまでぬくぬくと過すごした。I wrapped myself in the soft bedding with a hot-water bottle and, knowing nothing of the cold outside, stayed snug and warm until morning.
  • 外そとで働はたらく人ひとをよそに、自分じぶんだけ暖あたたかい部屋へやで毛布もうふに包つつまれてぬくぬくしている。Others are out working in the cold while he alone sits in a warm room, wrapped in a blanket, snug as can be.

ぶよぶよ · to be flabby and soft

A body that has lost its firmness.

The 濁点 makes the softness unhealthy and heavy. Light ふわふわ is not its voiceless counterpart — that would be ふよふよ; ふわふわ differs in its second mora, not in a diacritic.

Examples

  • 運動不足うんどうぶそくで、お腹なかの肉にくがつまめるほど重おもく柔やわらかくなってぶよぶよだ。For want of exercise my belly has gone heavy and soft enough to take hold of.
  • 久ひさしぶりに鏡かがみを見みたら、締しまりのない、重おもくて柔やわらかいぶよぶよの体からだになっていた。I looked in the mirror after a long time: my body had gone slack, heavy and flabby.
