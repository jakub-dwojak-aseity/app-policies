Japanese verbs change their endings to show politeness, tense and negation. Almost every verb belongs to one of two regular groups, godan and ichidan, and only する and 来る are truly irregular. Once you know which group a verb is in, a handful of rules gives you every form on this page.

The chart below shows the five forms you meet first: the dictionary form, polite ます, the te-form, the plain past た and the plain negative ない.

Godan verbs (group 1)

A godan verb changes its last kana by moving it to another row of the kana chart. For ます the last sound moves to the い-row: 書く → 書きます, 読む → 読みます, 話す → 話します. For ない it moves to the あ-row: 書く → 書かない, 待つ → 待たない. Verbs ending in う take わ, not あ: 買う → 買わない. One verb breaks the pattern: ある → ない, never あらない.

A verb that does not end in る is always godan. Verbs ending in -iru or -eru are usually ichidan, but some only look like it: 帰る and 入る are godan, so their te-forms are 帰って and 入って. Verbs ending in -aru, -uru or -oru, such as 取る, are godan too, apart from the irregular する and 来る.

The plain past た always follows the te-form: swap て for た and で for だ. If you know a verb's te-form, you know its past.

Ichidan verbs (group 2)

An ichidan verb drops る and adds the ending, with no sound changes at all: 食べる → 食べます, 食べて, 食べた, 食べない; 見る → 見ます, 見て, 見た, 見ない. This is exactly where it parts company with a godan verb ending in る, which doubles the next sound instead: 帰る → 帰って.

Irregular verbs: する and 来る

する becomes し before most endings: する → します, して, した, しない. Verbs built on する, such as 勉強する (to study), follow it exactly: 勉強する → 勉強して.

来る keeps its kanji but changes its vowel, so watch the reading: 来くる → 来きます, 来きて, 来きた, 来こない. Its ば-form is 来くれば.

The polite set: ます, ません, ました, ませんでした

ます has its own small set of endings, and they are the same for every verb: 書きます (I write), 書きません (I don't write), 書きました (I wrote), 書きませんでした (I didn't write). Build the ます-form once and the other three follow.

Negative forms: ない, なかった, なくて

Once you have the ない-form, it behaves like an い-adjective: drop い and add かった for the past or くて for the te-form. 書かない gives 書かなかった and 書かなくて; 食べない gives 食べなかった and 食べなくて.

Conditionals: ば and たら

For ば, a godan verb moves its last kana to the え-row and adds ば: 書く → 書けば, 話す → 話せば. An ichidan verb drops る and adds れば: 食べる → 食べれば. する gives すれば and 来る gives 来くれば.

たら is simpler still: take the plain past and add ら. 書いた gives 書いたら, 食べた gives 食べたら and した gives したら.

Every verb form, one by one

dictionary form

the base shape every other form is built from

書かく → 書かく · 食たべる → 食たべる · する → する · 来くる → 来くる

The plain form you find in a dictionary. Every other form is built from it.

the bare verb: write

How it is formed

  • every class alikeThis is the dictionary form itself — the shape every other form is built from.

Examples

  • 毎日まいにち、日記にっきを書かく。I write in my diary every day.

masu-stem

what is left of the polite form once its ending comes off

書かく → 書かき · 食たべる → 食たべ · する → し · 来くる → 来き

What is left when ます comes off. A great deal of grammar attaches right here.

where the rest attaches

How it is formed

  • godan verbIn a godan verb the final kana moves to the い row: く→き, む→み, る→り.

Examples

  • 手紙てがみを書かきながら、音楽おんがくを聞ききます。I listen to music while I write a letter.

polite ます form

the everyday polite shape for people you do not know well

書かく → 書かきます · 食たべる → 食たべます · する → します · 来くる → 来きます

The everyday polite form, used with people you do not know well.

I write (polite)

How it is formed

  • godan verbIn a godan verb the final kana moves to the い row, then the polite ending follows.
  • ichidan verbDrop る and add the polite ending.
  • する compound verbする becomes し, then the polite ending follows.

Examples

  • 毎朝まいあさ、新聞しんぶんを読よみます。I read the newspaper every morning.

polite negative ません

politely: that something does not happen

書かく → 書かきません · 食たべる → 食たべません · する → しません · 来くる → 来きません

The polite way to say something does not happen.

I don't write (polite)

Examples

  • 肉にくは食たべません。I don't eat meat.

polite past ました

politely: that something already happened

書かく → 書かきました · 食たべる → 食たべました · する → しました · 来くる → 来きました

The polite way to say something already happened.

I wrote (polite)

Examples

  • 昨日きのう、友達ともだちが来きました。A friend came over yesterday.

polite past negative ませんでした

politely: that something did not happen

書かく → 書かきませんでした · 食たべる → 食たべませんでした · する → しませんでした · 来くる → 来きませんでした

The polite way to say something did not happen.

I didn't write (polite)

Examples

  • 兄あには週末しゅうまつ、何なにもしませんでした。My older brother didn't do anything at the weekend.

plain negative (nai-form)

plainly, without the polite ending: it does not happen

書かく → 書かかない · 食たべる → 食たべない · する → しない · 来くる → 来こない

The plain way to say something does not happen.

I don't write

How it is formed

  • godan verbIn a godan verb the final kana moves to the あ row, then ない follows. Verbs ending in う take わ, not あ.
  • ichidan verbAn ichidan verb drops る and adds ない — not らない, which is what a godan verb ending in る would take.
  • する compound verbする becomes し, then ない follows.

Examples

  • 今日きょうはバスを待またない。歩あるいて行いく。I'm not waiting for the bus today. I'll walk.

plain past (ta-form)

plainly, without the polite ending: it happened

書かく → 書かいた · 食たべる → 食たべた · する → した · 来くる → 来きた

The plain past. It follows exactly the same sound changes as the te-form.

I wrote

Examples

  • 妹いもうとは昨日きのう、この本ほんを読よんだ。My younger sister read this book yesterday.

plain past negative

plainly, without the polite ending: it did not happen

書かく → 書かかなかった · 食たべる → 食たべなかった · する → しなかった · 来くる → 来こなかった

The plain way to say something did not happen.

I didn't write

Examples

  • 弟おとうとは子こどものころ、野菜やさいを食たべなかった。As a child, my younger brother didn't eat vegetables.

te-form

links one clause to the next and carries most requests

書かく → 書かいて · 食たべる → 食たべて · する → して · 来くる → 来きて

The connecting form. It links clauses together and carries most requests.

…and then…

How it is formed

  • godan verbA godan verb ending in ぐ takes いで — the same change as く, but the voicing carries over into で.
  • godan verbA godan verb ending in く takes いて. The consonant drops out and leaves い behind.
  • godan verbA godan verb ending in む, ぶ or ぬ takes んで. The ending voices as well: て becomes で.
  • godan verbA godan verb ending in す takes して. This is the one ending where nothing contracts.
  • godan verbA GODAN verb ending in う, つ or る takes って — a small tsu doubling the next sound. An ichidan verb ending in る does something else entirely: 食べる gives 食べて.
  • い-adjectiveDrop い and add くて.
  • ichidan verbAn ICHIDAN verb drops る and adds て. No sound changes at all — and this is exactly where it parts company with a godan verb ending in る, which would give って.
  • な-adjectiveAdd で. Not くて — that belongs to い-adjectives.
  • する compound verbする becomes し, then て or た follows.

Examples

  • ここに名前なまえを書かいてください。Please write your name here.

negative te-form

links clauses when the first half is a negative

書かく → 書かかなくて · 食たべる → 食たべなくて · する → しなくて · 来くる → 来こなくて

The connecting form when the first half is a negative.

I don't write, and…

Examples

  • 誰だれも来こなくて、寂さびしかった。Nobody came, and I felt lonely.

conditional ba-form

a condition: if this happens, something follows

書かく → 書かけば · 食たべる → 食たべれば · する → すれば · 来くる → 来くれば

If this happens, then something follows.

if I write…

How it is formed

  • godan verbIn a godan verb the final kana moves to the え row, then ば follows.
  • い-adjectiveDrop い and add ければ.
  • ichidan verbDrop る and add れば.
  • な-adjectiveAdd なら. The ば ending is optional and slightly formal.
  • する compound verbする becomes すれ, then ば follows.

Examples

  • ゆっくり話はなせば、わかります。If you speak slowly, I'll understand.

conditional tara-form

a condition once met: when this has happened, something follows

書かく → 書かいたら · 食たべる → 食たべたら · する → したら · 来くる → 来きたら

Once this has happened, then something follows.

once I've written…

Examples

  • 家いえに帰かえったら、電話でんわします。I'll call you when I get home.

Adjective forms

These two forms belong to adjectives, not verbs. An い-adjective swaps い for く to say how something is done: 高い → 高く. The one exception is いい, which works from its older form よい: いい → よく. A な-adjective adds に instead (静か → 静かに) and puts な directly before a noun: 静かな部屋, a quiet room. Adjectives also have te- and ば-forms; their rules are listed with those forms above.

adverbial form

says how something is done

高たかい → 高たかく · 静しずか → 静しずかに

Turns an adjective into a description of how something is done.

quickly, quietly

How it is formed

  • い-adjectiveDrop い and add く.
  • な-adjectiveAdd に. Not く — that belongs to い-adjectives.

Examples

  • 図書館としょかんでは静しずかに話はなしてください。Please speak quietly in the library.

attributive な form

describes the noun that comes right after it

静しずか → 静しずかな

The shape a na-adjective takes directly in front of a noun.

a quiet street

How it is formed

  • な-adjectiveAdd な. This is the ending the whole class is named after.

Examples

  • 静しずかな部屋へやで勉強べんきょうしたい。I want to study in a quiet room.
