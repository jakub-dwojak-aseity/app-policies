First day at a new job: you walk up to your colleagues at their desks

です/ます plus the set phrase お願ねがいします — humble in itself (お〜する), but everyday and unremarkable. A team is not a client: the 申もうし上あげます option reaches for a form that raises the person addressed, and would build a distance nobody asked for.

Examples

  • 田中たなかです。よろしくお願ねがいします。

A close university friend you have known since first year asks about lunch

Plain speech, 普通ふつう体からだ — and here that is the right answer, even though this whole section is about register. Keigo would be a step backwards: it would say “we are not that close”, which nobody meant.

Examples

  • うん、行いく。

A waiter sets the coffee down

いたす lowers the speaker towards the listener, while お〜 directs the action at the guest and raises them — the form does both at once, so it belongs to both groups at once (the character 兼けん here means “and”: 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ兼けんⅡ). That is what it is called in 敬語けいごの指針ししん, the 2007 guidelines of Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs, on which this app is based. In the category list such forms sit under Ⅰ. This is how service speaks to a guest, and it is heard daily in Japan.

Examples

  • お待またせいたしました。

Asking a lecturer about a deadline

です/ます plus すみません to open. For a student–lecturer relationship this is the right measure and nothing more is needed.

Examples

  • すみません、締しめ切きりはいつですか。

The opening line of an email to a client you already work with

The opening formula for an **ongoing** relationship; in correspondence like that it is written every time. It does two things at once: お世話せわになる raises the person whose care we are in — the reader — while おります adds modesty towards that same reader. If you are writing to someone for the first time, the formula does not fit: a first letter opens with 突然とつぜんのご連絡れんらく失礼しつれいいたします, “forgive me for writing out of the blue”.

Examples

  • いつもお世話せわになっております。

Your first week in a new team: you ask a colleague you met three days ago for a document

In a company ます stays even with someone your own age, and お願ねがいします is a set, courteous request. An office is 内うち towards the world, but it is not a home. What separates it from the お願ねがい申もうし上あげます option is 申もうし上あげる, not お願ねがい itself.

Examples

  • これ、お願ねがいします。

Answering the company phone

でございます is the most formal 丁寧ていねい語ご: it raises the tone of the utterance, not a person. The company name is not lowered here — the whole utterance is lifted towards the caller.

Examples

  • はい、山田商事やまだしょうじでございます。

A club senior asks about practice

A 先輩せんぱい gets ます and that is all. The relationship is close but not equal, and that is exactly where です/ます lives.

Examples

  • はい、行いきます。

Asking a passer-by for directions

です/ます with どちら instead of どこ. A stranger in the street is not a client: the politeness should be ordinary, not professional.

Examples

  • すみません、駅えきはどちらですか。

Asking your boss about a document, in-house

Inside the company your boss is a superior and takes the honorific. **The same boss will not take it in front of a client**, because there he stands on our side of the 内うち/外そと line — your own people against outsiders — which we come back to at level 5.

Examples

  • 部長ぶちょうはもうご覧らんになりましたか。

A message to your sister

Siblings are the innermost 内うち there is. Any ます here would read as a quarrel or a joke.

Examples

  • 今いまどこ。

In a shop, asking the assistant to show you a watch from the display case

すみません、見みせてもらえますか — ですます and もらえますか are entirely enough, and that is 丁寧語ていねいご. 見みせていただけますか, with 謙譲語けんじょうご (the form that lowers your own action), is correct too and ordinary in a shop; what goes wrong is stacking humility, not the fact that it is the customer speaking.

Examples

  • すみません、この時計とけいを見みせてもらえますか。

A senior teammate you are not on first-name terms with invites you out after work

すみません at the front and んです at the end — the politeness here is 丁寧語ていねいご. The reason needs no spelling out: 都合つごうが悪わるくて is enough, and that is how a refusal is made in Japanese.

Examples

  • すみません、その日ひはちょっと都合つごうが悪わるくて、行いけないんです。
