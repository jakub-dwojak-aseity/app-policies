いらっしゃる · to go, come or be — said of the other person

いらっしゃる is the honorific form of 行いく・来くる・いる. It describes the other person's action and raises that person. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 明日あしたは何時なんじにいらっしゃいますか。

おっしゃる · to say — said of the other person

おっしゃる is the honorific form of 言いう. It describes the other person's action and raises that person. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • お名前なまえは何なんとおっしゃいますか。

召めし上あがる · to eat or drink — said of the other person

召めし上あがる is the honorific form of 食たべる・飲のむ. It describes the other person's action and raises that person. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 何なにを召めし上あがりますか。

くださる · to give to me, or to do something for me — said of the other person

くださる is the honorific form of くれる. It describes the other person's action and raises that person. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • いつも手伝てつだってくださいます。

ご存ぞんじだ · to know — said of the other person

ご存ぞんじだ is the honorific form of 知しっている. It describes the other person's action and raises that person. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • この店みせをご存ぞんじですか。

お休やすみになる · to go to sleep — said of the other person

お休やすみになる is the honorific form of 寝ねる. This is the お〜になる pattern applied to 休やすむ, so the ending is regular. The one thing to memorise is the verb: someone's sleep is spoken of with 休やすむ, not 寝ねる.

Examples

  • 何時なんじにお休やすみになりますか。

伺うかがう · to go to someone, or to ask them something — said of oneself

伺うかがう is the humble form of 行いく・来くる・聞きく. It describes one's own action and raises **the person it is directed at** — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. Its twin 参まいる (謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ) describes the same act but raises nobody. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 明日あした、三時さんじに伺うかがいます。

申もうし上あげる · to say to someone one respects

申もうし上あげる is the humble form of 言いう. It describes one's own action and raises **the person it is directed at** — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. Its twin 申もうす (謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ) describes the same act but raises nobody. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • お礼れいを申もうし上あげます。

拝見はいけんする · to look at — said of oneself, humbly

拝見はいけんする is the humble form of 見みる. It describes one's own action and raises **the person it is directed at** — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 資料しりょうを拝見はいけんします。

いただく · to receive, eat or drink — said of oneself

いただく is the humble form of もらう・食たべる・飲のむ. This entry asks about the もらう sense, the one in the example sentence. It lowers your own receiving and by doing so raises the giver — which is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. In eating and drinking there is no giver and so nobody to raise: the same form then belongs to 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ, like まいる and 申もうす.

Examples

  • 部長ぶちょうに資料しりょうをいただきます。

さしあげる · to give to someone one respects

さしあげる is the humble form of あげる. It lowers your own giving and by that raises the person you give to — ordinary 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ, and it may be used towards the person you are speaking to. What needs care is something else: the auxiliary 〜てさしあげる. Applied to something done for someone it sounds like pointing out your own favour, so instead of 荷物にもつを持もってさしあげます you say お持もちします.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいにはこちらからお花はなをさしあげます。

お目めにかかる · to meet someone of higher standing

お目めにかかる is the humble form of 会あう. It describes one's own action and raises **the person it is directed at** — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 来週らいしゅうお目めにかかります。

まいる · to go or come — said modestly of oneself

まいる is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of 行いく・来くる. It describes one's own action modestly **towards the listener**, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • ただいままいります。

申もうす · to say, or to be called — said modestly of oneself

申もうす is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of 言いう. It describes one's own action modestly **towards the listener**, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 田中たなかと申もうします。

いたす · to do — said modestly of oneself

いたす is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of する. It describes one's own action modestly **towards the listener**, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 準備じゅんびはこちらでいたします。

おる · to be — said modestly of oneself

おる is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of いる. It describes one's own action modestly **towards the listener**, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorised.

Examples

  • 明日あしたは一日いちにち社しゃにおります。

存ぞんじる · to know or think — said modestly

存ぞんじる is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of 知しる・思おもう. It describes one's own knowing modestly **towards the listener**, raising nobody — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. When what is known is a person one respects, the form changes: 先生せんせいを存ぞんじ上あげております. 存ぞんじ上あげる raises that person and belongs to 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

Examples

  • ありがたく存ぞんじます。
