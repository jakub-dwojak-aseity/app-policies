In Japanese you don't put a number straight in front of a noun. You attach a counter to the number — a short word that says what kind of thing you are counting: 本 for long, thin things, 枚 for flat ones, 匹 for small animals. The number and the counter then fuse into a single word, and that is where the sound changes happen. 三 on its own is さん, but three pencils are 3本（さんぼん）; 六 is ろく, but six pencils are 6本（ろっぽん）.

The counted thing usually comes first, followed by the number with its counter and then the verb: ペンを3本（さんぼん）買いました — I bought three pens.

The patterns behind the changes

Most of the irregular readings come from 1, 6, 8 and 10. Before a counter starting with k or h, all four shrink into a small っ: 1個（いっこ）, 6個（ろっこ）, 8本（はっぽん）, 10匹（じゅっぴき）. Before s, only 1, 8 and 10 do: 1冊（いっさつ）, 8冊（はっさつ）, while 6冊（ろくさつ） stays whole. With 8 the full はち is sometimes heard as well: 8軒（はっけん・はちけん）.

Counters starting with h usually change their own first sound as well. After a small っ, h becomes p: 1本（いっぽん）, 6匹（ろっぴき）. After 3 it usually becomes b: 3本（さんぼん）, 3匹（さんびき）, and 4 usually leaves it alone: 4本（よんほん）. A few counters go their own way: 分 (minutes) gives さんぷん for 3 and よんぷん for 4, so learn those one by one. After 3, a few counters starting with k voice too: 3軒（さんげん）, and 階 (floors) gives さんがい. Counters starting with m, like 枚, never change: 1枚（いちまい）, 6枚（ろくまい）.

人 is irregular at the start: 1人（ひとり）, 2人（ふたり）, and from three on the regular にん: 3人（さんにん）. Note 4人（よにん）, not よんにん.

Which counter do I use?

つ works for most objects when you don't know the specific counter, but not for people or animals. Long, thin things such as pens, bottles and umbrellas take 本. Flat things such as paper, tickets and shirts take 枚. Small animals take 匹, books and magazines take 冊, and people take 人. Every counter on this page is explained in more detail below, with two example sentences each.

つ · things (general)

Anything with no counter of its own, and plenty of things that have one. The safest thing to say when you are not sure.

This is the native Japanese series, and it runs out at ten. From eleven you switch to the Chinese-derived numbers with no counter at all.

Examples

  • りんごを3つみっつください。Three apples, please.
  • いすが8つやっつあります。There are eight chairs.

人 にん · people

People, in any situation.

One and two are separate words built on the native series, and four takes よ — never よん.

Examples

  • 家族かぞくは4人よにんです。There are four of us in the family.
  • 教室きょうしつに2人ふたりいます。There are two people in the classroom.

個 こ · small solid objects

Small compact things: eggs, apples, stones, boxes, pieces of something.

The first counter where one, six, eight and ten cut short before it.

Examples

  • たまごを6個ろっこかいました。I bought six eggs.
  • 石いしが1個いっこおちていました。There was one stone lying there.

円 えん · yen

Japanese currency.

Nothing changes except four, which is よ.

Examples

  • この本ほんは1500円せんごひゃくえんです。This book is 1,500 yen.
  • おつりは40円よんじゅうえんです。Your change is forty yen.

番 ばん · number (in an order)

Position in a sequence: seat numbers, platform numbers, your turn.

ばん itself never changes, at any number — only the seven is said both ways, ななばん and しちばん. That is worth noticing on purpose: not every counter changes, and expecting a change everywhere is its own mistake.

Examples

  • わたしの席せきは23番にじゅうさんばんです。My seat is number 23.
  • 3番さんばんホームで待まっています。I am waiting on platform number 3.

本 ほん · long, thin things

Pens, bottles, umbrellas, trees, roads — anything long and narrow. Also train services, phone calls and shots on goal.

ほん turns into ぼん after three, and into ぽん after one, six, eight and ten.

Examples

  • ビールを3本さんぼんください。Three beers, please.
  • かさを2本にほんわすれました。I left two umbrellas behind.

枚 まい · flat, thin things

Sheets of paper, plates, shirts, tickets, stamps, slices of bread.

Nothing changes at any number — まい starts with m, and m never cuts short.

Examples

  • きっぷを2枚にまいかいました。I bought two tickets.
  • 紙かみが6枚ろくまいたりません。We are six sheets of paper short.

冊 さつ · bound volumes

Books, magazines, notebooks — anything bound along one edge.

Six does not cut short here: ろくさつ, not ろっさつ. Compare 六本 ろっぽん.

Examples

  • 本ほんを6冊ろくさつかりました。I borrowed six books.
  • ノートが8冊はっさつあります。There are eight notebooks.

匹 ひき · small animals

Cats, dogs, fish, insects — animals you could pick up.

The same shape as 本: ひき becomes びき after three and ぴき after one, six, eight and ten.

Examples

  • ねこが3匹さんびきねています。Three cats are sleeping.
  • きんぎょを8匹はっぴきかっています。I keep eight goldfish.

頭 とう · large animals

Cows, horses, elephants, bears — animals bigger than you.

Six leaves this one alone as well: ろくとう.

Examples

  • 牧場ぼくじょうに牛うしが10頭じゅっとう・じっとういます。There are ten cows on the farm.
  • 馬うまを1頭いっとうかっています。They keep one horse.

羽 わ · birds

Birds, and rabbits.

This counter began with an h sound and is halfway through losing it, so three, six, eight and ten are genuinely said both ways. Both are shown; neither is drilled.

Examples

  • にわとりが2羽にわいます。There are two chickens.
  • すずめが5羽ごわとまっています。Five sparrows are perched there.

台 だい · machines and vehicles

Cars, bicycles, computers, washing machines, cameras.

だい already starts with a voiced sound, so there is nothing left to change.

Examples

  • 車くるまが3台さんだいとまっています。Three cars are parked there.
  • パソコンを1台いちだいかいました。I bought one computer.

杯 はい · cupfuls and glassfuls

What is in the cup, not the cup itself: coffee, water, beer, bowls of rice.

Same family as 本 and 匹. Eight really is said both ways: はっぱい and はちはい. Six is ろっぱい, like ろっぽん and ろっぴき — ろくはい is marginal, so it is accepted rather than taught.

Examples

  • コーヒーを1杯いっぱいのみました。I drank a cup of coffee.
  • 水みずを2杯にはいください。Two glasses of water, please.

着 ちゃく · garments

Suits, coats, dresses, kimono — whole outfits rather than single pieces.

ちゃく starts with the ch sound, which cuts short after one, eight and ten but not six.

Examples

  • スーツを1着いっちゃくかいました。I bought one suit.
  • コートが8着はっちゃくあります。There are eight coats.

足 そく · pairs of footwear

Shoes, socks, sandals — always a pair, never one shoe.

Three is said both ways and sources disagree about which is standard, so it is shown rather than asked.

Examples

  • くつしたを2足にそくかいました。I bought two pairs of socks.
  • くつが10足じゅっそく・じっそくならんでいます。Ten pairs of shoes are lined up.

軒 けん · houses and shops

Buildings counted as premises: houses, shops, restaurants, bars.

One of the two k-counters that voice after three: さんげん. Compare 三回 さんかい.

Examples

  • この通とおりに店みせが3軒さんげんあります。There are three shops on this street.
  • 家いえを1軒いっけんたてました。They built one house.

名 めい · people (formal)

People, in restaurants, on forms and in announcements. The polite counterpart of 人.

Unlike 人, this one is completely regular: いちめい, にめい, さんめい, よんめい.

Examples

  • 4名よんめいですが、席せきはありますか。There are four of us — do you have a table?
  • 会議かいぎに20名参加さんかしました。Twenty people took part in the meeting.

曲 きょく · pieces of music

Songs and instrumental pieces — one track, one 曲.

A k-counter that stays plain after three: さんきょく, like 三回 and unlike 三階.

Examples

  • すきな歌うたを3曲さんきょくえらびました。I picked three songs I like.
  • コンサートで6曲ろっきょくひきました。They played six pieces at the concert.

人前 にんまえ · portions of food

Servings in a restaurant or a recipe: two portions of gyoza, a recipe for four.

Built on 人, but one and two do not take ひとり and ふたり: the usual portions are いちにんまえ and ににんまえ. ひとりまえ is heard for one portion as well, and that same reading carries a second meaning — someone grown up and good at their job. Four keeps the よ of 四人: よにんまえ, and nine is きゅうにんまえ, never くにんまえ.

Examples

  • ぎょうざを2人前ににんまえください。Two portions of gyoza, please.
  • このレシピは4人前よにんまえです。This recipe is for four.

皿 さら · helpings on a plate

What is on the plate, not the plate itself — plates are counted with 枚. Sushi at a conveyor-belt shop, a second helping of curry.

One of the few counters that takes the old native numbers: ひとさら and ふたさら, with にさら heard as well. From three the plain numbers take over — さんさら, ろくさら, じゅっさら. みさら is in the dictionaries and is accepted, but it is not what you hear today. The eight is the one cell nobody settles: はっさら and はちさら are both in use and no source picks between them, so both are shown and neither is drilled — the same answer as at 三足 and at four cells of 羽.

Examples

  • おすしを3皿食たべました。I ate three plates of sushi.
  • カレーを1皿ひとさらおかわりしました。I had another plate of curry.

割 わり · tenths, ten percent

Proportions in tenths: 一割 is ten percent, 三割 is thirty. Shop signs count discounts this way — 三割引き is thirty percent off.

Nothing changes at any number, and the series stops at ten by arithmetic: 十割 is the whole thing, so there is no eleventh tenth.

Examples

  • このシャツは3割引びきでした。This shirt was thirty percent off.
  • 学生がくせいは全体ぜんたいの4割よんわりです。Students are forty percent of the total.

丁目 ちょうめ · chōme, a block in an address

The numbered block inside a district: Ginza 4-chōme. Japanese addresses go from the large to the small, and this is the step below the district name.

The same shape as 着: ちょ cuts short after one, eight and ten — いっちょうめ, はっちょうめ, じゅっちょうめ — but not after six.

Examples

  • 銀座ぎんざ4丁目よんちょうめにすんでいます。I live in Ginza 4-chōme.
  • バスていは8丁目はっちょうめにあります。The bus stop is in 8-chōme.
