Privacy Policy

Kuzushi · version 1.6 · last updated October 15, 2026

Kuzushi teaches you to recognize and produce variants of the same message in casual Japanese. Learning, reviews and progress work on your device offline. Optional AI help sends text only when you request it. From version 1.6, anonymous usage statistics operate only with your consent.

No account or advertising. AI help and voluntary statistics are separate features. Declining statistics does not limit learning, purchases or AI help. Your own sentences and exercise answers are not sent in statistics.

Your account

There isn't one. Kuzushi does not ask you to register, sign in or provide an email address, and it has no way to identify you.

Where your learning data goes

Your answer history, course progress, review dates, self-assessments and personal notes stay on your device. Anonymous usage categories sent only with consent are described separately below. Answer texts and notes are not sent in statistics.

Export in Settings → Data creates a file you save wherever you choose. A version 1.6 backup includes progress, history, settings, personal notes, local sentence lists and saved AI results. The app does not upload that file. The statistics queue and local permission state are excluded from the export.

Text and saved results in the “Sentences” tab do not provide evidence of mastery. They remain local, separate from study history. You can delete them in that tab; resetting study progress does not remove personal lists. A photo used for scanning is read on the phone and is not uploaded. AI help receives only text you select and confirm with Show full form or Show levels.

Data shared with the developer's other apps

Kuzushi belongs to a family of Japanese-learning apps. If you have more than one of them installed, the app shows a Your Japanese section with one row per app.

Here is how that works: Kuzushi writes a small card file on your device, in an area shared by the apps in this family. The card carries five things about your studying: how much of the material you have mastered, how many days your streak runs, how many sessions you have done, one sentence about your weakest spot (in Polish and in English), and whether today's goal is already met. The field for your next reviews is always left empty by Kuzushi, to preserve the existing shared-card format. New course review dates remain in Kuzushi. Plus three technical ones: which app wrote the file, in what format, and when. Nothing else. There is no answer history, no list of the patterns you practiced and nothing you type in the “Sentences” tab, and your progress cannot be reconstructed from this file.

The file never leaves your device, and it is read only by the other apps in this family that you have installed on that same device. Nothing syncs between devices.

Two things worth saying plainly. First, the shared area survives uninstalling a single app — the file goes only once you remove every app in the family from the device. Second, “Reset all progress” deletes this file too, along with your study progress. Personal notes and saved AI lists have separate deletion controls.

Reminders

The evening reminder is optional and entirely local. If you turn it on, iOS schedules the notification on the device; nothing is sent anywhere and no server is involved. If you decline the notification permission, the rest of the app works exactly as before.

Tracking and advertising

There is no advertising, advertising identifier (IDFA), third-party analytics SDK or crash reporter. We do not sell data or track you across apps or websites. Voluntary statistics use our own server; AI help operates as described separately below.

Network connections

The App Store handles purchases and restores. AI help connects to api.jd-japanese.pl when you use it and when checking the remaining allowance or subscription entitlement. The text of a line is sent only when you tap Show full form or Show levels.

Separately, only with consent to statistics, the app sends anonymous event batches and retries failed uploads when returning to the app, regaining connectivity or entering the background. Learning works without these connections. Lack of App Attest disables AI help, but voluntary statistics do not require it.

Voluntary anonymous usage statistics

Measurement is off by default. Only after consent do we record events and send them to https://api.jd-japanese.pl/v1/events. They help us assess the learning flow, returns to reviews and purchase offers. We do not reconstruct activity before consent.

Events cover first entry after consent, active days, completing or abandoning a session, the first completed learning and review sessions, returning on a later study day, viewing an offer and a completed purchase. They contain only closed categories and ranges: session kind and outcome, answer counts, session duration, general question types and, on abandonment, the last question type, answer outcome and consecutive mistake count range. Completion describes participation, not fluency or language mastery; speaking self-assessment is not pronunciation assessment.

We include the app name, interface language, schema version, current app version and feature variant, plus the version and variant frozen at consent. Cohort information contains the week of first launch, study-day number and entry category: fresh installation, restored progress or later consent. An active day includes a range of session counts, a general learning-stage category and flags for AI use, seeing an offer, Premium access, an AI subscription and reminders. Stage is neither a JLPT level nor an assessment of proficiency. A purchase event contains only the purchase kind. AI use is only a flag in an active-day snapshot; we do not send a separate event with the AI feature kind or outcome.

There is no installation, device or person identifier. Each event has its own random UUID solely for deduplication. We send no question or answer text, AI sentences, notes, lesson or task identifiers, email address, location, device model, iOS version, App Attest identifiers or Apple transaction identifiers. Events are not linked to AI help data or across apps. We use no cookies, advertising or profiling.

An IP address is necessary for the connection, but we do not record it in events or access logs. Separate abuse protection uses a keyed IP hash, removed after 24 hours of inactivity. Offline, the local queue waits for at most 30 days, up to 500 events. It is excluded from progress backups. Acknowledged events are removed from the queue. Raw events stay on the server for up to 400 days, followed only by anonymous daily sums without UUIDs.

You can withdraw consent in Settings → Privacy → Send anonymous usage statistics. Turning it off stops collection and delivery and deletes the local queue and local measurement state of current sessions. Already received events may remain until retention ends: without an identifier we cannot locate a particular person's data. Declining consent, a lack of internet and server outages do not change learning or access to purchased features.

The “Sentences” tab — optional AI help

The “Sentences” tab is optional. Without it the app teaches and tracks progress exactly the same way. It has two modes, each with its own list: “Full form” — a line you heard, say in an anime or a drama, comes back in full form, without contractions, with each contraction named — and “As spoken” — your own sentence in full form comes back the way it is shortened in speech, at levels 1, 3 and 5 of this app. Politeness stays as it was: this is not a rendering into the plain form.

When you tap Show full form or Show levels, we send two things to our server at api.jd-japanese.pl: the line or sentence you typed (at most 120 characters) and the language the answer should come back in (Polish or English, following the interface language). The server passes them to a language model from OpenAI, and the answer comes back to the app. Nothing else is sent — not your progress, not your answer history, not the photo, and nothing that could identify you.

We do not store the text of your line — it goes into no database and no log; nor do we store the model's answer. The database has no column that could hold either, and adding one breaks an automated test on the server. Our request to OpenAI asks it not to retain the response. The only exception is short-lived and never touches disk: a finished answer stays in the server's memory for two minutes, so that tapping the button twice on the same line does not count as two requests.

What we do keep, because the feature cannot work without it:

  • an App Attest key identifier with its public key — a technical identifier from Apple's mechanism confirming that a request comes from a genuine, unmodified copy of this app. It is generated on the device, is unique to this one app, and cannot be linked to anything Apple or another app can see;
  • the subscription's transaction identifier from the App Store — only if you subscribe: so we know you have it, including after a reinstall or on another device signed in with the same Apple Account;
  • usage counters — how many requests were made today and this month.

For a shorter time we keep technical billing and maintenance data: Apple transaction identifiers and request metrics (request number, feature name, model, status, token count, response time) — for 90 days. We do not store your IP address; for abuse protection the server keeps only a keyed hash of it, for 24 hours.

When it connects, the app also attaches a one-time DeviceCheck token issued by iOS. For Kuzushi the server does not use it and does not store it: the free sample here is not protected against reinstalls, so the token has nothing to guard.

None of this is linked to your name, email address or an account — because the app does not know them. We do not use any of it for tracking, profiling or advertising. There is no account to delete; if you want these records gone, write to me.

Purchases

The one-off Premium purchase and the AI Help subscription are handled entirely by Apple. Kuzushi never sees your payment details, card number or billing information. The app reads the state of your purchases from iOS. While the AI Help subscription is active, the app passes our server the Apple-signed proof of that transaction — only so the server can check the signature itself and grant the subscription's requests. We do not send the Premium transaction proof to our server. Only with consent to statistics may we send the kind of completed purchase and Premium access category, without a receipt, product or transaction identifier.

How Apple processes data is described in Apple's privacy policy.

Reporting a problem

The Suggest a feature and Report a problem buttons in Settings prepare an email you can read and edit before sending. Nothing is sent until you tap “Send” yourself. A problem report comes pre-filled with the app version, the build number, the iOS version and the device model identifier (e.g. iPhone14,3) — never a device identifier. You can delete those lines before sending. If you write, I will see your email address and your message; they are used only to reply.

Children

The app collects no data that could identify anyone, of any age: there is no account and it does not ask for a name, age or address. For the “Sentences” tab we keep only the technical identifiers and counters described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. The address of this page carries the app's version number, so older releases always point to the document that actually described them — releases before 1.5, which connect to no server at all, are described by the document at the address without a version number.
