Voicing pairs: き–し – Japanese mimetics

さらさら and ざらざら, きらきら and ぎらぎら – five pairs from き to し where 濁点 changes the state of things, not the sound.

  • きらきら · to sparkle
  • ぎらぎら · to glare
  • くしゃくしゃ · to be crumpled
  • ぐしゃぐしゃ · to be squashed to a pulp
  • くるくる · to spin nimbly
  • ぐるぐる · to go round and round
  • さらさら · to be smooth and dry
  • ざらざら · to be rough
  • しとしと · to drizzle quietly
  • じとじと · to be damp and clammy

きらきら · to sparkle

Stars, dew in the morning sun.

Voiceless き breaks the light into small points and leaves it pleasant to the eye — ぎらぎら is that same brightness pushed until you look away. ぴかぴか does not break the light into points: it is a polished surface giving the light back evenly (shined shoes), or a light that comes on and goes off as a whole (稲光がぴかぴか光る); きらきら is many points glittering at once.

Examples

  • 夜空よぞらで小ちいさな星ほしが明あかるく光ひかってきらきらと輝かがやいている。Small stars shine brightly in the night sky.
  • 小ちいさな水滴すいてきが朝日あさひを受うけて明あかるく光ひかってきらきらしている。Small droplets catch the morning sun and glitter brightly.

ぎらぎら · to glare

Midsummer sun, neon at night.

Voiced ぎ does not make the light bigger — it pushes it past the limit; you look away. きらきら stays with the small points that are pleasant to look at, and ぴかぴか describes a clean surface giving the light back evenly (polished shoes); neither of them glares.

Examples

  • 真夏まなつの太陽たいようが、目めを開あけていられないほど朝あさから夕方ゆうがたまで休やすみなくぎらぎらと照てりつける。The midsummer sun beats down from morning to evening without a break, too bright to keep your eyes open.
  • 街まちの看板かんばんの光ひかりが、目めを開あけていられないほど夜よるじゅう消きえずにぎらぎらしている。The light of a street sign never goes out all night and glares too hard to keep your eyes open.

くしゃくしゃ · to be crumpled

A screwed-up sheet, a receipt balled up in a fist.

Voiceless く measures the force of a hand: a sheet squeezed in your fist takes しわ and is still a sheet. The creases on their own are しわしわ — an unironed shirt is しわしわ although nobody crushed it — while くしゃくしゃ always reports an action, something balled up in a hand. There is no verdict in it — くしゃくしゃの笑顔 is warm, and a crumpled receipt bothers nobody; it takes ぐしゃぐしゃ to crush hard enough that the shape breaks up. Note: くしゃくしゃする belongs to a face and a mood (顔をくしゃくしゃにして笑う, 気分がくしゃくしゃする); of a sheet of paper you say くしゃくしゃだ or くしゃくしゃになる.

Examples

  • メモ用紙ようしは、指先ゆびさきで軽かるく握にぎるだけでたやすくつぶれ、一度いちどもんだだけで丸まるい塊かたまりになって、もうくしゃくしゃだ。The note gives without resisting at the lightest squeeze between the fingers and after a single crumple it is already a round little ball — completely crumpled.
  • 一度いちど丸まるめただけの紙かみは、指ゆびで軽かるく押おせばすぐへこむくしゃくしゃの塊かたまりで、机つくえの上うえに転ころがっていても誰だれも気きに留とめない。A sheet balled up in a single motion is a crumpled lump that dents at a light press of the finger, and nobody gives it a second look lying there on the desk.

ぐしゃぐしゃ · to be squashed to a pulp

A cardboard box under a boot.

Voiced ぐ does not change the action, only its force: くしゃくしゃ leaves creases, ぐしゃぐしゃ presses hard enough for the shape to give way (強く押しつぶされて形が崩れる). That is why a boot, a wheel and a weight belong here, and not fingers alone. It adds no size — 小さな紙をぐしゃぐしゃに丸める is perfectly ordinary. Nor does the 濁点 itself bring water: ぐしゃぐしゃ is wet as readily as dry (雨で道がぐしゃぐしゃになる, ぐしゃぐしゃに潰れた車). Being soaked through is ぐっしょり — wet to the last layer, but with the shape intact; ぐしゃぐしゃ always rules on a shape that has given way. Note: ぐしゃぐしゃする is a different thing — a squelch inside a wet shoe (靴の中がぐしゃぐしゃする) or a head in a muddle; of a crushed box you say ぐしゃぐしゃだ or ぐしゃぐしゃになる.

Examples

  • 段だんボール箱ばこが体重たいじゅうをかけて踏ふみつけられ、靴くつの下したでつぶれて、もとの形かたちが見分みわけられないほどすっかりぐしゃぐしゃだ。The cardboard box, stamped on with the full weight of a body, was crushed under the boot until there was no telling what shape it had been.
  • 重おもしの下したで押おしつぶされた紙束かみたばは、一枚いちまいずつの見分みわけもつかないほど形かたちが崩くずれ、すっかりぐしゃぐしゃの状態じょうたいになった。The stack of paper, pressed flat under a weight, lost its shape until you could no longer tell one sheet from another.

くるくる · to spin nimbly

A spinning top, a pinwheel, a reel.

Voiceless く makes the turn small: the circle fits in your hand, where ぐるぐる traces a circuit you have to walk round. A top spins fast because it is small, not because it lacks 濁点 — voicing changes no tempo in this pair.

Examples

  • 小ちいさな独楽こまが机つくえの上うえで、見みていて楽たのしくなるほど速はやくくるくると回まわっている。A small spinning top whirls on the desk so quickly it is a pleasure to watch.
  • 小ちいさな糸巻いとまきが毛糸けいとを巻まき取とりながら、見みていて飽あきないほど速はやくくるくると回まわっている。A small reel winds up the yarn, spinning so quickly you never tire of watching.

ぐるぐる · to go round and round

A Ferris wheel, a tenth lap of the field.

Voiced ぐ widens the circle: the くるくる turn that fitted in your hand becomes a circuit. It does not slow it — 目がぐるぐる回る is fast. くらくら turns nothing: it is how someone feels when the room spins on them, not a movement you can watch. うろうろ traces no circle either — it is walking back and forth, with no plan and no centre.

Examples

  • 大おおきな観覧車かんらんしゃが、朝あさから休やすまずぐるぐると回まわっている。The big Ferris wheel has been turning since morning without a moment's pause.
  • 大おおきな水車すいしゃが川かわの水みずを受うけて休やすみなくぐるぐると回まわり続つづける。A big waterwheel takes the river's flow and keeps turning without a break.

さらさら · to be smooth and dry

Hair, sand, a stream — dry and smooth.

Voiceless さ leaves the surface smooth: the hand runs over it without catching. What separates it from ざらざら is what the hand feels, not how strongly — there the hand catches. すべすべ is smooth too, but it is about skin the hand glides over and rules nothing about dryness, whereas さらさら is always dry and loose (hair, sand, a stream), which is why a baby's skin is すべすべ. さわさわ is not touch at all, but the faint rustle of leaves.

Examples

  • 洗あらったばかりの髪かみは乾かわいていて空気くうきを含ふくみ、指ゆびを通とおすとさらさらする。Freshly washed hair is dry and full of air; fingers slip right through it.
  • 乾かわいて空気くうきをよく含ふくむ滑なめらかな砂すなは、握にぎっても固かたまらずに指ゆびの間あいだからさらさらと落おちる。Dry, airy, smooth sand will not clump in your fist and runs through your fingers.

ざらざら · to be rough

A surface you do not want to run your hand over.

Voiced ざ turns smooth into rough: sandpaper instead of freshly washed hair, so さらさら and ざらざら sit at the two ends of one axis. That is the usual direction — but 濁点 is a strong tendency, not a law, and some pairs shift a completely different axis. The remaining neighbours differ not in degree but in what they are about: でこぼこ is unevenness big enough to see with the eye, shaping the thing itself (a stone wall, a yard), while ざらざら is a grain you feel with a fingertip on a surface that is otherwise flat; ごわごわ rules on the stiffness of a towel that will not bend, not on its grain; かさかさ on thin, dried-out skin; がさがさ mostly on the loud rustle of coarse paper; and じゃりじゃり on loose grit that shifts under a tooth or in a shoe, not on a surface.

Examples

  • 紙かみやすりの表面ひょうめんは硬かたくて粗あらくて、触さわるとざらざらする。The surface of sandpaper is hard and rough, and you feel it the moment you touch it.
  • 乾かわいたセメントの床ゆかは硬かたくて粗あらくて、素足すあしで踏ふむとざらざらだ。A dried-out concrete floor is hard and rough, and a bare foot feels it.

しとしと · to drizzle quietly

Fine rain that falls on and on.

しとしと says HOW it rains: slowly, finely, on and on — and that “on and on” is what pushes ぽつぽつ aside, since ぽつぽつ names the first separate drops on the pavement, still countable and still no promise of rain. ザーザー stands at the other end: a downpour you can hear through a closed window, while しとしと is not heard at all. It makes no pair with じとじと — that describes the state of the air and cannot rain (じとじと降る is not Japanese).

Examples

  • 細こまかい雨あめが、やむ気配けはいもなく朝あさからゆっくりしとしとと降ふっている。Fine rain has been falling slowly since morning, with no sign of stopping.
  • 春はるの細こまかい雨あめが、夜よるまで途切とぎれずに屋根やねをぬらしながらゆっくりしとしとと降ふり続つづける。The fine spring rain falls slowly right into the night, wetting the roof without a break.

じとじと · to be damp and clammy

The air in the rainy season, a sweat-soaked shirt on your back.

じとじと describes heavy, muggy damp that hangs in the air and settles on things — and that is all it does. Rain falling is しとしと: a fine, quiet drizzle that goes on and on (しとしとと雨が降る). The test is one sentence long: しとしとと降る is ordinary Japanese, while じとじと降る is not — じとじと does not rain, it stays, on your skin, on the walls and in the washing. So it is not an intensified しとしと but a second word built on the same kana skeleton.

Examples

  • 梅雨つゆのあいだじゅう空気くうきが重おもく、干ほした洗濯物せんたくものが乾かわかないほどじとじとだ。All through the rainy season the air is heavy and so clammy that the hung-out laundry will not dry.
  • 汗あせを吸すったシャツが背中せなかに張はりつき、重おもく湿しめったまま一日いちにちじゅう乾かわかずにじとじとする。The sweat-soaked shirt clings to his back and will not dry all day — heavy with damp and clammy.
