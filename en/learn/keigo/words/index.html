Honorific and humble words – keigo

いらっしゃる, 召し上がる, 伺う – twenty honorific and humble words, each beside its plain verb and inside a sentence.

  • くださる · to give to me, or to do something for me — said of the other person
  • ご存ぞんじだ · to know — said of the other person
  • お休やすみになる · to go to sleep — said of the other person

いらっしゃる · to go, come or be — said of the other person

The office tells a visitor where the teacher is.

“Go, come or be” when speaking of someone you honor. いらっしゃる replaces 行いく・来くる・いる; its polite form is いらっしゃいます.

Memorize this form: it does not follow from adding a regular ending. For your own side, “go/come” becomes 伺うかがいます or まいります; “be” becomes おります.

いらっしゃいます means “is” here. The form honors the teacher whose presence you describe.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいは今いま、研究室けんきゅうしつにいらっしゃいます。The teacher is in their office now.

おっしゃる · to say — said of the other person

You tell a colleague what the teacher said.

おっしゃる is the honorific form of 言いう. It describes the other person's action and raises that person.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

おっしゃいました describes the teacher’s speaking. The past tense keeps the respect shown to the speaker.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいは「来週らいしゅうです」とおっしゃいました。The teacher said, “Next week.”

なさる · to do — said of the other person

You ask an older listener what exercise they do on their days off.

なさる is the honorific form of する. It describes the other person's action and raises that person.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

なさいます replaces the ordinary します. It honors the person exercising.

Examples

  • 休日きゅうじつはどんな運動うんどうをなさいますか。What exercise do you do on your days off?

ご覧らんになる · to look at — said of the other person

A tourist information staff member asks a visitor to look at a map.

ご覧らんになる is the honorific form of 見みる: it describes viewing by a person you honor. Memorize 覧らん in place of 見み.

The ending follows お／ご〜になる, as in お休やすみになる. ご accompanies 覧らん, a stem of Chinese origin, while お accompanies the native Japanese 休やすみ.

ご覧らん describes the visitor’s viewing respectfully. ください makes the sentence a polite request.

Examples

  • こちらの地図ちずをご覧らんください。Please look at this map.

召めし上あがる · to eat or drink — said of the other person

A hotel staff member says that the guest will have breakfast.

召めし上あがる is the honorific form of 食たべる・飲のむ. It describes the other person's action and raises that person.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

召めし上あがります describes the guest’s eating. It honors the eater, not the speaker.

Examples

  • お客様きゃくさまは朝食ちょうしょくを召めし上あがります。The guest will have breakfast.

くださる · to give to me, or to do something for me — said of the other person

You say that the teacher gave you a book.

くださる is the honorific form of くれる. It describes the other person's action and raises that person.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

くださいました is the past form of くださる. The teacher is the giver and the person you honor.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいが本ほんをくださいました。The teacher gave me a book.

ご存ぞんじだ · to know — said of the other person

You politely ask whether the listener knows the way to the station.

ご存ぞんじだ is the honorific form of 知しっている. It describes the other person's action and raises that person.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

ご存ぞんじです describes the listener’s knowledge and honors the person who knows the way.

Examples

  • 駅えきまでの道みちをご存ぞんじですか。Do you know the way to the station?

お休やすみになる · to go to sleep — said of the other person

In the evening, you explain that the teacher is already asleep.

お休やすみになる describes sleeping by a person you honor. It uses 休やすむ instead of 寝ねる with the regular お〜になる pattern.

Memorize the change of verb rather than a new ending.

お休やすみになる describes the teacher’s sleep respectfully. 〜ています marks the ongoing state here.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいはもうお休やすみになっています。The teacher is already asleep.

伺うかがう · to go to someone, or to ask them something — said of oneself

A receptionist politely asks a guest for their address.

伺うかがう humbly describes your going, coming, or asking/listening: 行いく・来くる・聞きく. It lowers the doer and honors the person the action is directed at — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. For “go/come”, まいる describes your own side courteously toward the listener, without honoring the person you go to — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ. まいる does not replace 聞きく.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

伺うかがいます means “I will ask” here, not “I will come”. It humbly describes your asking and honors the person asked.

Examples

  • ご住所じゅうしょを伺うかがいます。I will ask for your address.

申もうし上あげる · to say to someone one respects

You offer the listener formal congratulations.

申もうし上あげる is the humble form of 言いう. It describes one's own action: it lowers the doer and thereby raises the person it is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. Its twin 申もうす (謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ) describes the same act but raises nobody.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

申もうし上あげます humbly describes my words. I honor the person I congratulate.

Examples

  • 心こころからお祝いわいを申もうし上あげます。I offer my heartfelt congratulations.

拝見はいけんする · to look at — said of oneself, humbly

The teacher showed you their photographs. You say that you looked at them.

拝見はいけんする is the humble form of 見みる. It describes one's own action: it lowers the doer and thereby raises the person it is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

拝見はいけんしました humbly describes my viewing. It honors the teacher whose photographs I view.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいの写真しゃしんを拝見はいけんしました。I looked at the teacher’s photographs.

いただく · to receive, eat or drink — said of oneself

When thanking the teacher, you mention a book you received from them.

いただく means receive, eat, or drink. This entry uses “receive”: it humbly describes your receiving and honors the giver — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

When いただく describes eating or drinking without referring the action to a giver, it functions as 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ: it presents your own side courteously to the listener. When you emphasize receiving something from a respected person, you honor the giver — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. The meaning “eat/drink” alone does not rule out such a person.

いただきました describes my receiving the book. The humble form honors the teacher as the giver.

Examples

  • 先生せんせいから本ほんをいただきました。I received a book from the teacher.

さしあげる · to give to someone one respects

A shop staff member offers a customer a product sample.

さしあげる is humble あげる: it describes your giving and honors the recipient — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ. You may use it toward the listener. Adding 〜てさしあげる to an action describing a favor needs care: it can sound like pointing out your favor.

Instead of 荷物にもつを持もってさしあげます, say お持もちします.

さしあげます humbly describes giving by our side. The customer is the recipient you honor.

Examples

  • お客様きゃくさまに見本みほんをさしあげます。I will give the customer a sample.

お目めにかかる · to meet someone of higher standing

You introduce yourself to the listener at your first meeting.

お目めにかかる is the humble form of 会あう. It describes one's own action: it lowers the doer and thereby raises the person it is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

お目めにかかります humbly describes my meeting. It honors the person I meet.

Examples

  • 初はじめてお目めにかかります。I am meeting you for the first time.

承うけたまわる · to hear (e.g. a request) or to accept (e.g. an order, a task) — said of oneself

A hotel staff member says they will take a guest’s reservation.

承うけたまわる is the humble form of 聞きく・引ひき受うける. It describes one's own action: it lowers the doer and thereby raises the person it is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

承うけたまわります humbly describes the staff member’s taking a reservation. It honors the guest making it.

Examples

  • ご予約よやくを承うけたまわります。I will take your reservation.

まいる · to go or come — said modestly of oneself

You formally tell the listener about your trip to Osaka.

まいる is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of 行いく・来くる. It describes one's own action modestly towards the listener, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

まいります describes my journey modestly when speaking to the listener. It honors no person or place I go to.

Examples

  • 来月らいげつ、大阪おおさかへまいります。I will go to Osaka next month.

申もうす · to say, or to be called — said modestly of oneself

A receptionist introduces themselves to a guest.

申もうす is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of 言いう. It describes one's own action modestly towards the listener, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

申もうします introduces my name modestly toward the listener. It does not honor the recipient of the words as the target of the action.

Examples

  • 受付うけつけの鈴木すずきと申もうします。My name is Suzuki; I work at reception.

いたす · to do — said modestly of oneself

A staff member tells a customer that their team will do the checking.

いたす is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of する. It describes one's own action modestly towards the listener, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

いたします describes our side’s action modestly toward the listener. It does not honor a person the action is directed at.

Examples

  • 確認かくにんはこちらでいたします。We will handle the checking.

おる · to be — said modestly of oneself

On the phone, you tell the listener where they can find you.

おる is the humble (丁重ていちょう語ご) form of いる. It describes one's own action modestly towards the listener, but raises nobody the act is directed at — that is what makes it 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご.

The form follows from no rule — it has to be memorized.

おります modestly describes my presence toward the listener. It honors no particular person or place.

Examples

  • 私わたしは受付うけつけにおります。I am at reception.

存ぞんじる · to know or think — said modestly of oneself

In a formal conversation, you give your opinion about a proposed method.

存ぞんじる modestly expresses “I know” or “I think/feel” toward the listener — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅡ・丁重ていちょう語ご. It honors nobody. When saying you know a person you respect, use 存ぞんじ上あげる — 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ.

存ぞんじております is a humble “I know”. For a respected person: 先生せんせいを存ぞんじ上あげております.

存ぞんじます means “I think” here. You express your opinion modestly toward the listener without honoring another person.

Examples

  • その方法ほうほうがよいと存ぞんじます。I think that method is good.
