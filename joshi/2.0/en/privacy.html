Privacy Policy

Joshi · documents 2.0 · last updated November 21, 2026

Joshi is an app for practicing Japanese particles. All of the learning — the section map, rounds, checkpoints and progress — happens on your device and works offline. Optional AI help in the “My sentences” tab sends a sentence to our server only when you tap a button. Since version 1.5.1 the app has voluntary anonymous usage statistics, off by default. Since version 2.0.0 you can also send a report from the answer card, and an idea or a bug description through a form in Settings; you give an email address there only if you want a reply. This policy covers Joshi 2.0.0; version 1.5.1 uses the earlier policy.

No account and no advertising. AI help sends the sentence you type and the answer language; the server does not store its text. Anonymous usage statistics send only categories, number ranges and flags, after your consent. A report from the answer card and a message from the form leave only when you tap “Send”. “My sentences”, your answer history and full progress stay on your device.

Your account

There is none. The app does not create a profile and does not sign you in anywhere; installing and using it requires no personal details. The only exception is an email address you may type into the feedback form yourself if you want a reply — see Feedback form.

Where your study data goes

Your progress, the state of the section map, answer history, the result of the starting test, day streak, notes on roles and settings are stored on your device only. Your full history and progress records are not sent to the server; with your consent we send only the anonymous summaries described below. A report from the answer card, if you send one, contains a copy of that one question. If you export a backup (Settings → Your data → Progress backup → Export progress), the resulting file is yours — it is written wherever you choose to save it, and the app does not upload it.

The “My sentences” tab is a notebook: sentences saved from a question card, typed in yourself or written after a round, together with the latest AI help result, if there is one. The notebook lives on your device only and works offline. What you type into it, and the answer that comes back from AI help, do not feed your progress. Since version 2.0.0 the notebook is part of the progress export, and an import adds its sentences to the ones already on the device. You delete sentences in “My sentences”, one at a time or all at once; they also go when you delete the app. “Reset progress” in Settings does not touch the notebook — the two are separate things.

You can add a sentence from an AI help result as an example to a particle role — it then appears under “Your examples” on that role's page. These examples also stay on your device only, in a separate file that is not part of the progress export and is deleted neither by “Reset progress” nor by deleting sentences from “My sentences”. You remove them on the role's page.

You can also scan a sentence from a photo (with the camera or from your photo library) — from a page, subtitles or a speech bubble, for example. The text is recognized on the phone; the photo is never saved or sent anywhere — only the text you pick and correct goes into the field, and it reaches the server only when you tap Explain with AI or Check with AI. The app asks for camera access the first time you take a photo; the photo library opens through the system picker, which gives the app only the photo you choose.

Data shared with the developer's other apps

Joshi belongs to a family of Japanese-learning apps. Your path has a Your Japanese tab with one row for each app in this family that you have on the device.

Here is how that works: Joshi writes a small card file on your device, in an area shared by the apps in this family. The card carries five things about your studying: how much of the material you have mastered, how many days your streak runs, how many sessions you have done, one sentence about your weakest spot (in Polish and in English), and whether today's goal is already met. The field for your next reviews is always left empty by Joshi, because it does not schedule reviews by date. Plus three technical ones: which app wrote the file, in what format, and when. Nothing else. There is no answer history, no list of the roles you practiced and nothing you type in “My sentences”, and your progress cannot be reconstructed from this file.

The file never leaves your device, and it is read only by the other apps in this family that you have installed on that same device. Nothing syncs between devices.

Two things worth saying plainly. First, the shared area survives uninstalling a single app — the file goes only once you remove every app in the family from the device. Second, “Reset progress” deletes this file too, along with the rest of your progress.

Reminders

The reminder about today's goal is optional, off by default and entirely local: one a day, at the time you choose, only on a day that is not done yet, and never in vacation mode. If you turn it on, iOS schedules the notification on the device; nothing is sent anywhere and no server is involved. With consent to statistics, only a flag indicating enabled reminders is sent. If you decline the notification permission, the rest of the app works exactly the same.

Tracking and advertising

None. There is no third-party analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporter and no advertising identifier (IDFA) in the app. It does not track you across other apps or websites, and it does not sell data. Usage statistics, answer-card reports and feedback-form messages are processed on our own server; data for AI help is passed on as described below.

Network connections

The app connects to our server for AI help, with consent for usage statistics, and when you send a report from the answer card or a message from the feedback form. Traffic for purchases and restoring purchases is handled by iOS and the App Store. For AI help, the app connects only at these moments: when you open a sentence from “My sentences” in the AI help editor, the AI help subscription screen or Settings (to show how many requests are left); when you tap Explain with AI or Check with AI, and straight afterwards, to refresh that number; when you tap Try again; and after you buy the subscription or restore purchases (so the server learns about the subscription). AI help connections do not run in the background or on a timer. The text of a sentence is sent only when you tap Explain with AI or Check with AI — or Try again after a failed attempt. A sentence that already has a result in the same mode is not sent a second time: the app shows the saved result.

With consent, statistics send batches of events and retry failed uploads after you give consent, at launch and when the app returns to the foreground, when it goes to the background, when the connection comes back and at the start of a new study day. A report from the answer card and a message from the form connect to the server only when you tap Send; opening either screen sends nothing.

On a device that does not support Apple's App Attest, there are no AI help buttons and the app does not use online AI help; “My sentences” then works as a plain notebook. Voluntary usage statistics, answer-card reports and the feedback form do not require App Attest.

Voluntary anonymous usage statistics

Measurement helps us find out whether studying is useful and where learners stop practicing. It is off by default and is not required to use the app. Only after your consent do we record events and send them to our server at api.jd-japanese.pl. Consent to statistics is separate from using AI help.

  • First entry after consent; completing or dismissing onboarding, only if you consented while onboarding was still in progress. Earlier events are not reconstructed.
  • An active day: a range of session counts, the learning section 1–5 — the number of the section on the map that holds the role you met most recently, with sections 5 and 6 counted together as 5 (not a JLPT level) — and flags for using AI, seeing an offer, full-version access, an AI help subscription and enabled reminders.
  • Completing or abandoning a session (a round, a checkpoint or a “Timed” round): its kind, outcome, ranges of answer counts and AI uses, and general types of questions shown with count ranges. For an abandoned session, also the last question's type, its outcome (correct, incorrect or unanswered), and a range for consecutive mistakes. We do not send questions, answers or particle role identifiers.
  • Learning milestones: the first completed session of new material and a second completed session on a different study day. Joshi does not schedule SRS reviews and sends no first SRS review event.
  • Viewing an offer and its general trigger; a completed purchase — only its kind (full version or AI help subscription), without a product or transaction identifier.
  • AI use — feature kind and outcome: success, refusal or error; no sentence text or model answer.

Events also include the cohort week (the week of first consent), day since its start, interface language, app version and experiment variant at consent and currently, and a general entry category: fresh installation, restored progress or later consent. There is no installation, device or person identifier. Each event has its own random UUID solely to avoid counting it twice when delivery is retried. We collect no texts, answer history, content or role identifiers, email address, location, device model or iOS version. Statistical events contain no account token, App Attest identifiers, purchase identifiers or the purchase-attempt UUID described below. Statistics use no cookies and on the server are not linked to AI help data, reports or feedback-form messages. An attempt is associated with a purchase only locally to confirm its origin; the purchase event sent still includes only its kind.

An IP address is necessary for the connection, but we do not record it in events or access logs. A separate abuse protection counter uses a keyed hash of the address; it is periodically removed after 24 hours of inactivity and is not linked to events. Offline, events wait on the device for at most 30 days, up to 500 events. The queue is excluded from backups and progress exports. Acknowledged events are removed from the queue. Raw events stay on the server for 400 days, then are replaced by anonymous daily sums without UUIDs. We do not use statistics for advertising, profiling or tracking across apps.

We ask for consent at the end of onboarding and, in an installation updated from an earlier version, once if it has not been answered before. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Your data, using Send anonymous usage statistics. Turning it off cancels delivery, deletes the local queue and the current session record used for measurement, as well as local purchase-attempt records; it stops collection and future uploads. It does not remove a token already stored in an Apple transaction. Events already received may remain until retention ends: without an installation identifier we cannot locate a particular person's events and delete them on request. Declining consent, a lack of internet or a server outage does not change learning, progress or access to purchased features.

Reports from the answer card — since version 2.0.0

Below the answer card there is a Report a problem link. Reporting is voluntary. Opening the report screen sends nothing. Only tapping Send passes the data to our server at https://api.jd-japanese.pl/v1/issue-reports. Before sending you can preview the question, your answer and the explanation shown (Content included in the report), you choose a reason and you can add a comment. You need no account, no purchase and no consent to usage statistics. Sending, a failed send or giving up does not change your grade or learning progress.

A report contains the reason you chose, an optional comment (up to 2,000 characters) and a copy of one question: the exercise type, the sentence identifier, the particle role and confusable pairs it concerns, the content version (the app version with its build number), the instruction, the sentence with its gap, the choices offered, your answer, the expected answer, the grading outcome and the explanation shown — including the expandable “More” part, even if it was not expanded. We also include the app name, its version and build number, and a language code: the language chosen in Settings (e.g. en) or, if none was chosen, the device's language and region (e.g. en_GB). Your answer and comment may contain your own text — do not put personal or confidential information in them.

We include no person, installation, device or advertising identifier, no email address, no answer history, no learning progress and nothing from “My sentences”. Each report gets a random UUID so that retrying the same send does not store it twice. The UUID identifies the report, not you; the server also records the time it was received.

The content goes into a separate report register, available to authorized staff in a secured panel, and is used only to check and fix the material or the grading. It does not go into usage statistics or to the AI model provider. We delete the question copy, answer, comment and staff text notes 180 days after the report is received, in a daily automatic cleanup. So that an unresolved problem does not drop out of the queue, we keep a minimal record without that content: the UUID and app, the reason chosen, dates, status, priority, links to our task list or to another report, the planned and resolved version, and the history of changed fields. The record stays until the case is closed and for 180 days after closing. Deleting the app or turning off statistics does not delete reports already sent.

The connection reveals your IP address to the server. Abuse protection limits the number of sends (20 in 10 minutes) using a counter based on a keyed hash of that address, not linked to the report content; the limit entry is deleted after 24 hours. Technical logs contain metadata such as the route, status, handling time and request identifier, not the report content.

Feedback form: “Suggest a feature” and “Report a problem” — since version 2.0.0

In Settings → Help you will find Suggest a feature and Report a problem. Both open a form inside the app. Opening the form sends nothing. Only tapping Send passes the message to our server at https://api.jd-japanese.pl/v1/feedback. The form requires no account, no purchase and no consent to usage statistics. This is a different channel from the Report a problem link below the answer card: that one carries a copy of the question, this one only what you type and the data in the Sent with your message block.

A message contains its kind (idea or bug), a topic if you choose one, your text (up to 2,000 characters) and, for a bug, an optional description of what happened before. In the Sent with your message block you see the rest before sending: the app name, its version and build number, and the interface language. That field contains the language code: the language chosen in Settings (e.g. en) or, if none was chosen, the device's language and region (e.g. en_GB). The Include system and model switch is on by default and adds the iOS version and the phone model identifier (e.g. iPhone14,3) — a series name shared by millions of units, not a device identifier. You can turn it off. Each message gets a random UUID so that retrying the same send does not store it twice; the server also records the time it was received. We include no person, installation, device, advertising or purchase identifier, and no learning history or progress. Your text may contain whatever you type — do not put confidential information in it.

Email address — only if you want a reply. You give an address only after turning on the I’d like a reply switch. Turning it on means you agree that we may write back to that address about this one message. We do not add the address to any mailing list or use it for anything else. Without the switch on, the app does not send the address, and the server rejects an address sent without that consent. The address does not stay in the app: unsent text waits on the device as a draft, the address and the reply switch do not.

The message goes into a separate register, available to authorized staff in a secured panel. The email address is stored in a separate field, apart from the message content, and the panel shows it only when a specific message is opened, never in the list. The message does not go into usage statistics or to the AI model provider, and its content and address are not written to server logs.

How long. We keep the address for 180 days from receipt, as long as the message text, then delete it automatically. In the same daily automatic cleanup, 180 days after receipt, we delete the message text, the description of what happened before the bug, the app version, build number, language, iOS version and phone model. So that an unresolved case does not drop out of the queue, a minimal record without that data remains: the UUID and app, kind and topic, dates, status, priority, links to our task list or to another message, the planned and resolved version, and the history of changed fields. The record stays until the case is closed and for 180 days after closing. You can ask for earlier deletion by writing to joshi@jd-japanese.pl. It is enough to give the email address used in the form or to describe the message so we can find it. If we reply to your address, that correspondence stays in our mailbox like any ordinary email; you can ask us to delete it too.

The connection reveals your IP address to the server. Abuse protection limits the number of sends (20 in 10 minutes) using a counter based on a keyed hash of that address, not linked to the message content; the IP address itself is not stored in the register. The technical logs described for answer-card reports apply to the form as well.

“My sentences” — optional AI help

AI help is optional. Without it the app teaches and tracks progress exactly the same way, and “My sentences” works as a notebook. It has two modes: “Explain” — someone else's sentence, from a manga, subtitles or a photo for example, comes back with the role of each particle, named after a role from this app — and “Check mine” — your own sentence comes back with each particle marked right, wrong, or “works, but…”, with a short why and the particle to use.

When you tap Explain with AI or Check with AI, we send two things to our server at api.jd-japanese.pl: the sentence you typed (at most 120 characters) and the language the answer should come back in (Polish or English, following the interface language). The server passes them to a language model from OpenAI, and the answer comes back to the app. The AI request content does not include your progress, answer history or photo. Technical identifiers needed for this feature are described below; statistics do not use them.

We do not store the text of your sentence — it goes into no database and no log; nor do we store the model's answer. The database has no column that could hold either, and adding one breaks an automated test on the server. Our request to OpenAI asks it not to retain the response. The only exception is short-lived and never touches disk: a finished answer stays in the server's memory for two minutes, so that tapping the button twice on the same sentence does not count as two requests.

What we do keep, because the feature cannot work without it:

  • an App Attest key identifier with its public key — a technical identifier from Apple's mechanism confirming that a request comes from a genuine, unmodified copy of this app. It is generated on the device, is unique to this one app, and cannot be linked to anything Apple or another app can see;
  • the subscription's transaction identifier from the App Store — only if you subscribe: so we know you have it, including after a reinstall or on another device signed in with the same Apple Account;
  • usage counters — how many requests were made today and this month.

For a shorter time we keep technical billing and maintenance data: Apple transaction identifiers and request metrics (request number, feature name, model, status, token count, response time) — for 90 days. We do not store your IP address; for abuse protection the server keeps only a keyed hash of it, for 24 hours.

When it connects, the app also attaches a one-time DeviceCheck token issued by iOS. For Joshi the server does not use it and does not store it: the free sample here is not protected against reinstalls, so the token has nothing to guard.

None of this is linked to your name, email address or an account — because the app does not know them. We do not use any of it for tracking, profiling or advertising. There is no account to delete; if you want these records gone, write to me — the transaction identifier from your App Store purchase history is enough to locate them.

Purchases

The one-off full-version purchase and the AI help subscription are handled entirely by Apple. Joshi never sees your payment details, card number or billing information. The app reads the state of your purchases from iOS. While the AI help subscription is active, the app passes our server the full Apple-signed proof of that transaction (JWS), so the server can check the signature itself and grant the subscription's requests. If the transaction contains the purchase-attempt UUID described below, that UUID also reaches the server as part of the proof. Proof of the full-version purchase is not sent to the server. An anonymous purchase event includes only its kind, without a purchase-attempt UUID or transaction identifier.

Only while consent to statistics is enabled, the app creates a separate random UUID for each local purchase attempt and passes it to Apple as appAccountToken. Despite the field's name, it is not an account, person or installation identifier and is not shared across different attempts. The app keeps a local association to confirm that a received transaction originated from that purchase attempt. The UUID is not included in statistical events. Apple stores it in the transaction; it may also remain in subscription renewal transactions. Withdrawing consent deletes the local record and stops future measurement, but does not delete or change an Apple transaction or the token stored in it.

How Apple processes data is described in Apple's privacy policy.

An email prepared in the Mail app

In one place Joshi still prepares an email in your device's Mail app: on the startup error screen, when the app cannot open its data. The message contains the error description plus the app version, build number, iOS version and device model identifier (e.g. iPhone14,3) — a name shared by millions of units, never a device identifier. Nothing is sent until you tap “Send” in the Mail app yourself, and you can change or delete any of the text before sending. If you write this way or directly to the app's address, I will see your email address and your message; they are used only to reply.

Crash reports

The app collects no crash reports of its own. If you have Apple analytics sharing turned on in iOS settings, the system may pass an anonymous report to the developer — that happens outside the app and is governed by Apple's privacy settings.

Children

The app requires no account and does not ask for a name, age or address. For AI help we keep the technical identifiers and counters described above; anonymous usage statistics are collected only with consent. A voluntarily sent answer, comment or form message may contain personal data if someone types it in; please don't. The email address in the form is optional and is used only to reply.
