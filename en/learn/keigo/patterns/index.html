Polite patterns – keigo

です/ます, お〜になる, 美化語 – eleven frames you lay over any word, each inside the sentence where it stands.

  • 行いきます — ます raises the tone, not a person
  • 学生がくせいです — です does what ます does, where there is no verb
  • 受付うけつけでございます — a step above です, still singles nobody out
  • お〜になる — the other person's action
  • お〜する — one's own action towards someone
  • ご〜になる — a Sino-Japanese stem
  • ご〜する — one's own action, Sino-Japanese stem
  • お + a native word
  • ご + a Sino-Japanese word
  • お茶ちゃ — beautifying the word itself
  • お〜いたす — one step above お〜する

行いきます — ます raises the tone, not a person

This is the zero point: everything keigo adds on top of the polite form itself is measured from 行いきます. ます says nothing about who performs the action — it singles nobody out; it lifts the tone of the whole utterance towards the person you are speaking to. That is why 行いきます works for yourself and for the listener alike — while 行いく without ます is not a grammatical mistake, only a sentence one tone lower. Forms that single someone out look different, and they come right after this one: you say いらっしゃいます when it is the listener who is going.

Examples

  • 明日あしたは九時くじに行いきます。

学生がくせいです — です does what ます does, where there is no verb

In an “X is Y” sentence です does exactly what ます does with a verb: it raises the tone, not a person. です is the lowest polite form in a sentence with no verb — which is why 学生がくせいだ sounds wrong to someone you have only just met, even though it contains no form that raises anybody. The lowest, but not the only one: でございます stands a step above it, and about a person you want to single out you say でいらっしゃいます.

Examples

  • 田中たなかさんは学生がくせいですか。

受付うけつけでございます — a step above です, still singles nobody out

でございます is a more courteous です and still singles nobody out. That is why shops and reception desks reach for it when speaking about things, about places and about their own side — that is, themselves, their company and their premises: こちらが受付うけつけでございます, 山田やまだでございます. The rule has another side, and it matters just as much: about a person you are singling out you do not say でございます — there でいらっしゃいます takes over (田中たなか様さまでいらっしゃいますか). About yourself it is the other way round and でございます stays: でいらっしゃいます would be singling yourself out.

Examples

  • こちらが受付うけつけでございます。

お〜になる — the other person's action

The verb stem goes between お and になる. The pattern covers the action of the person being raised — never one's own.

Examples

  • もうお読よみになりますか。

お〜する — one's own action towards someone

The same verb stem as in お〜になる, only the other way round: there you raise someone else's action, here you lower your own, directed at someone you respect.

Examples

  • 荷物にもつをお持もちします。

ご〜になる — a Sino-Japanese stem

The stem of this pattern is a Sino-Japanese noun (漢語かんご) that becomes an action with する: 利用りようする, 説明せつめいする, 確認かくにんする. Such a stem usually takes ご — ご利用りようになる, ご説明せつめいになる, ご確認かくにんになる. The exceptions sit in the word itself rather than in the pattern: a few long-settled 漢語かんご take お and keep it here as well — お電話でんわになる, お約束やくそくになる. Those have to be learned one at a time.

Examples

  • こちらをご利用りようになりますか。

ご〜する — one's own action, Sino-Japanese stem

The counterpart of お〜する for Sino-Japanese stems. Common in customer service: ご案内あんない, ご連絡れんらく, ご説明せつめい.

Examples

  • 会場かいじょうまでご案内あんないします。

お + a native word

A native (和語わご) noun takes お: お名前なまえ, お仕事しごと, お荷物にもつ. That same お does three different things, and what decides is not the object but whose side the sentence raises. When the thing belongs to the person being raised — お名前なまえ, the listener's name — it is 尊敬語そんけいご, the honorific. When you speak of your own action directed at someone (お手紙てがみを差さし上あげます), it is 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ, the humble form. And when the word raises nobody and merely lifts the tone — お金かね, お酒さけ — it is 美化びか語ご; お does not make the money belong to anyone.

Examples

  • お名前なまえをお願ねがいします。

ご + a Sino-Japanese word

A Sino-Japanese (漢語かんご) noun takes ご: ご連絡れんらく, ご住所じゅうしょ, ご意見いけん. The rule has exceptions to be learned separately: お電話でんわ, お時間じかん, お食事しょくじ.

Examples

  • ご連絡れんらくをお待まちしております。

お茶ちゃ — beautifying the word itself

Here お raises nobody and lowers nobody. It raises the word itself — which is why the official guidelines 敬語けいごの指針ししん (Keigo no shishin) give it a category of its own, 美化びか語ご, “beautification”, while textbooks fold it into the polite forms 丁寧ていねい語ご.

Examples

  • お茶ちゃをどうぞ。

お〜いたす — one step above お〜する

Two things at once: お〜する lowers one's own action towards the person it is directed at, and いたす adds courtesy towards the listener. 敬語けいごの指針ししん calls such forms 謙譲語けんじょうごⅠ兼けんⅡ. Hence their natural place in customer service and correspondence; in private talk they sound artificial.

Examples

  • 資料しりょうをお送おくりいたします。
