The sentence skeleton and particles – Japanese N5 grammar

は, が, を, に, で, と, も, の, や, だけ – fourteen particle roles that an ordinary Japanese sentence is built from.

  • は · the topic — the rest of the sentence is about it
  • が（新情報） · who or what: newly introduced
  • が（排他） · this one and no other, out of several
  • を（対象） · what the action covers: what is eaten, read, bought
  • を（通過・起点） · where the motion goes through or starts from
  • に（方向・相手） · where motion is headed, who receives the thing
  • で（場所） · the place of an action — something happens there
  • で（手段） · what something is done with: tool, means, material
  • と（並列） · and — a list of nouns, not examples
  • と（共同） · together with (a person)
  • も · too, also; not… either
  • の · whose, which, from where — noun beside noun
  • や · and, and the like — the list stays open
  • だけ · only, just

は · the topic — the rest of the sentence is about it

the topic — an announcement of what is being talked about

Marks what the sentence is about, and hands the rest of the sentence over to saying something about that topic. The topic is usually something the listener already knows. It is not the same as the subject: the topic is often the subject as well, but it can just as easily be the object or a time expression.

noun + は; written with the character は, but pronounced “wa”

Examples

  • 母ははは元気げんきです。My mother is well.Topic and subject coincide — the simplest case.
  • 今日きょうは寒さむいですね。It is cold today, isn’t it?The topic is a time expression, not the subject. The sentence does not say “the day is cold”, but “as for today — it is cold”.
  • この店みせは安やすいです。This shop is cheap.We point to a shop both speakers know, and only then say something about it.

が（新情報） · who or what: newly introduced

the subject presented as new information

Introduces a subject the listener does not yet have in mind. That is why the answer to a “who?” question always takes が — the answer itself is the new part. When it singles one item out of several, が does something else and has an entry of its own: が（排他）.

noun + が

Examples

  • あそこに猫ねこがいます。There is a cat over there.The cat comes up in the conversation for the first time, so it takes が, not は.
  • だれがこの絵えをかきましたか。Who painted this picture?A question about the subject. は never follows a question word.

が（排他） · this one and no other, out of several

the subject singled out as the only right one

Picks one item out of several and says it is this one, not another. It answers “which one?” rather than “what is new?” — which is why 私が行きます means “I am the one who will go”. When it introduces a new subject, が does something else and has an entry of its own: が（新情報）.

noun + が; when picking one out of several

Examples

  • 私わたしが行いきます。I’ll be the one to go.Out of several people, this one — hence が, not は. With は the sentence would only say what I intend to do.
  • この本ほんがいいです。I’d like this book.Out of several books the choice falls on this one — が says which. With は the sentence would be a general opinion about the book.

を（対象） · what the action covers: what is eaten, read, bought

the thing the action is directed at

Marks what the action covers: what is eaten, read, bought. It stands with transitive verbs — the ones that need something to act on. With verbs of motion を does something else and has an entry of its own: を（通過・起点）.

noun + を; written with its own character を, pronounced “o”

Examples

  • 毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のみます。I drink coffee every morning.The coffee is what undergoes the drinking — a textbook direct object.
  • 日本語にほんごの本ほんを読よみました。I read a book in Japanese.The whole phrase “book in Japanese” is a single object.

を（通過・起点） · where the motion goes through or starts from

the space a motion verb passes through or starts from

With verbs of motion を marks not the thing acted on but the space the motion covers: the one it passes through (道を歩く) or the one it starts from (家を出る, バスを降りる). These verbs are not transitive, so they take no object at all.

noun (place) + を + verb of motion

Examples

  • 道みちを歩あるきます。I walk along the street.The street is not what the action is directed at — を marks the space the motion passes through.
  • 毎朝まいあさ七時しちじに家いえを出でます。I leave the house at seven every morning.The house is where the motion starts. 出る is not a transitive verb, so there is no object here at all.

に（方向・相手） · where motion is headed, who receives the thing

the destination of movement or the recipient of an action

Shows where something is heading or who it is directed at. What matters here is the destination: the place where the movement ends, not the route to it.

noun + に

Examples

  • 来月らいげつ、京都きょうとに行いきます。Next month I am going to Kyoto.Kyoto is the point where the journey ends.
  • 友ともだちに手紙てがみを書かきました。I wrote a letter to a friend.The friend is the recipient, not a place — the same particle handles both cases.

で（場所） · the place of an action — something happens there

the place where an action takes place

Marks the stage on which something happens. The deciding question is whether something takes place there or someone is simply present — with bare existence (ある, います) に takes over. The same で comes back later in another role, marking the set within which something is the greatest: クラスで一番.

noun denoting a place + で

Examples

  • 図書館としょかんで勉強べんきょうします。I study in the library.The library is where the studying takes place.
  • 公園こうえんで子こどもが遊あそんでいます。Children are playing in the park.Playing is an action, so the park takes で, not に.

で（手段） · what something is done with: tool, means, material

a tool, a means of transport or a material

The same character as with place, but it answers a completely different question: not “where”, but “with what”. It covers tools, means of transport, languages and materials.

noun + で

Examples

  • はしで食たべます。I eat with chopsticks.The chopsticks are a tool, not a place.
  • 電車でんしゃで会社かいしゃへ行いきます。I go to work by train.A means of transport. This clashes with place: “I read on the train” would be written exactly the same way.

と（並列） · and — a list of nouns, not examples

joining the named nouns into one list

Joins nouns as the named members of a list rather than examples of a larger group, as や does. Context may suggest a complete list, but と alone does not prove that nothing else exists. A person accompanying an action instead belongs to と（共同）.

noun + と + noun

Examples

  • パンとたまごを買かいました。I bought bread and eggs.Bread and eggs are named as things bought. The sentence alone does not establish whether anything else was bought; や would present them as examples.
  • 兄あにと姉あねがいます。I have an older brother and an older sister.An older brother and an older sister are named. と alone does not establish that the speaker has no other siblings.

と（共同） · together with (a person)

the person the action is done with

Marks the companion in an action — someone doing it together with the speaker. The shape of the sentence does not settle it: パンとたまごを買いました and 妹と映画を見ました look the same. What settles it is the noun before と — a thing of the same kind as the next one belongs to a list, while a person taking part in the action is a companion. Listing is a separate entry: と（並列）.

noun for a person + と + verb

Examples

  • 妹いもうとと映画えいがを見みました。I watched a film with my sister.My sister was watching it with me, and a film cannot belong to the same list as a person — so here と points to who I was with.
  • 弟おとうとと公園こうえんで遊あそびました。I played with my little brother in the park.遊ぶ does not take に for a person — only と marks the companion.

も · too, also; not… either

adding another item on equal footing

Says that what was just said about something else applies here as well. It replaces は, が and を — it is not added to them, it takes their place.

noun + も (replacing は / が / を)

Examples

  • わたしも行いきます。I am going too.We do not say わたしはも — も takes the place of は.
  • 兄あには医者いしゃです。姉あねも医者いしゃです。My brother is a doctor. My sister is a doctor too.The second sentence repeats the predicate of the first, so the subject takes も.

の · whose, which, from where — noun beside noun

possession, or a closer description of a noun

Subordinates one noun to another. Its range is wider than the English possessive: it covers ownership, origin, material, field and membership of a group. The modifier always stands before what it modifies.

noun + の + noun

Examples

  • これはわたしの本ほんです。This is my book.The simplest use — ownership.
  • 日本語にほんごの先生せんせいに聞ききました。I asked the Japanese teacher.Here の marks a field, not an owner.

や · and, and the like — the list stays open

an open list — the items named are only examples

Lists things as examples of a larger group and leaves the list open. と joins the named members without this signal of exemplification, but does not itself prove that nothing else exists. や joins nouns, so activities must be expressed as nouns here.

noun + や + noun; など often comes at the end of the list

Examples

  • 机つくえの上うえに本ほんやノートがあります。There are books, notebooks and the like on the desk.Books and notebooks are examples of things on the desk. と would join them as the named members of a list; it would not itself prove that these are the only things on the desk.
  • 休やすみの日ひは買かい物ものや散歩さんぽをします。On my days off I do the shopping, go for a walk and so on.や joins nouns, which is why the activities appear here as 買い物 and 散歩, not as verbs.

だけ · only, just

narrowing to the thing named, with no judgment of amount

Narrows things down to what is named and implies nothing beyond that — it does not say whether that is a lot or a little. A preceding を usually drops out, while は and が can be placed after だけ: 水だけは飲みました. At higher levels しか〜ない restricts in a similar way, but adds a sense of not-enough.

noun + だけ; number + だけ; verb in dictionary form + だけ

Examples

  • 私わたしは新聞しんぶんを読よむだけです。I only read the newspaper.Here だけ limits the action: the only thing I do is read the newspaper.
  • 今日きょうは一人ひとりだけで行いきます。Today I will go alone.Here 一人だけで means going alone, without other people.
  • 今日きょうは水みず丈だけを飲のみます。Today I am drinking only water.Here 丈 is read だけ and limits the choice to water. This particle is usually written in kana as だけ; 丈 is an alternative spelling to recognize. It is not the noun たけ meaning length or height.
