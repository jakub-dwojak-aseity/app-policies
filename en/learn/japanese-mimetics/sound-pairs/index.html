Sound pairs: the か row – Japanese mimetics

からから, かちかち, かりかり – pairs from the か row that are a noise and a state at once; 濁点 thickens the sound itself.

  • がさがさ · to rustle coarsely
  • がたがた · to rattle and shake
  • かちかち · to be rock-hard; to click dry and high
  • がちがち · to clench so hard it will not let go — and to knock bluntly doing it
  • かちゃかちゃ · to clink
  • がちゃがちゃ · to clatter and bang
  • からから · to be bone-dry; to clatter dry and hollow
  • がらがら · to be empty — and to rattle hollow in that emptiness
  • かりかり · to crunch finely
  • がりがり · to scrape or gnaw

がさがさ · to rustle coarsely

A big paper bag, cardboard.

がさがさ is the loud rustle of coarse paper, a sound that gets into your ears. That is what separates it from ごそごそ: there the same hands shift the same things, only quietly, and it bothers nobody. ざらざら is not a sound at all — it is the grain of a surface under your finger. It is also used of chapped skin, in exactly the place かさかさ is, so this is not a pair that teaches the rule.

Examples

  • 大おおきな紙袋かみぶくろを抱かかえ直なおすたびに、粗あらい表面ひょうめんがこすれ合あい、耳みみざわりな音おとを部屋へやじゅうに響ひびかせてがさがさと鳴なる。Every time the big paper bag is shifted in the arms, its rough surface rubs against itself and rustles through the whole room, grating on the ear.
  • 大おおきな段だんボールの粗あらい縁ふちをこすると、耳みみにつく音おとが部屋へやの隅すみまで届とどいてがさがさと鳴なる。Rub the rough edge of a big cardboard box and the grating rustle carries into every corner of the room.

がたがた · to rattle and shake

A shelf unit in an earthquake, a heavy metal door.

Voiced が swaps the pot lid for a whole shelf unit: the same motion, now heavy, loud and unpleasant — かたかた stays a metre away, がたがた carries into the next room. The other neighbours are told apart not by force but by subject: がちゃがちゃ is the metallic clink of things knocking together (keys, a door handle), がらがら the emptiness and the hollow rattle that carries in it; ぐらぐら is the wobble of an unsteady thing and makes no sound, がくがく the shaking of joints, so knees rather than shelves; and でこぼこ names the unevenness of the road itself, while がたがた is what that road does to the cart.

Examples

  • 古ふるくて重おもい棚たなが、鈍にぶい音おとを隣となりの部屋へやまで響ひびかせながらがたがたと揺ゆれた。An old, heavy shelf unit shook, its dull rattle carrying all the way to the next room.
  • 重おもい金属きんぞくの戸とが、眠ねむれないほど鈍にぶい音おとで一晩ひとばんじゅうがたがたと鳴なり続つづけている。A heavy metal door keeps rattling dully all night long, and you cannot sleep through it.

かちかち · to be rock-hard; to click dry and high

Dried-out clay, frozen ground, a ticking clock.

Voiceless か is hardness itself and a high, clean click — two sides of one word: you know a block has dried right through because flicking it answers exactly like that. It settles nothing about size or weight — かちかちに凍った地面 is a whole frozen field. Nor about the actor: it works for things and for people alike (緊張でかちかちになる). The hardness it shares with がちがち, and hardness is not what decides between them — what kind of hardness it is, does. かちかち answers high and clear, so it takes a clock (時計がかちかち) and two pebbles flicked together; がちがち answers low and loud (歯ががちがち), and where there is no sound at all it rules on a clamping after which nothing moves — and there it reaches where かちかち does not: not stiffening as such, since 肩がかちかち and 緊張でかちかち are both fine, but what is unyielding by nature (がちがちの規則, 守りをがちがちに固める).

Examples

  • 乾かわいた空気くうきの中なかで、置おき時計とけいが、枕元まくらもとにだけ届とどく澄すんだ細ほそい音おとで、止とまることなくかちかちと時ときを刻きざむ。In the dry air the desk clock keeps time with a thin, clear sound that carries no further than the pillow, and it never stops.
  • 何なんか月げつも形かたちを変かえない粘土ねんどは、乾かわいて硬かたくなり、指ゆびではじくと耳元みみもとにだけ残のこる澄すんだ高たかい音おとでかちかちと鳴なる。The clay has not changed shape in months: it has dried so hard that a flick of a finger sets it ringing high and clear, no further than your ear.

がちがち · to clench so hard it will not let go — and to knock bluntly doing it

Teeth in the cold, a rule with no exceptions.

Voiced が adds force, and the force is both audible and visible. Audible: 歯ががちがち鳴る is a low, blunt, loud knock — かちかち is high and clear, and no amount of volume closes that gap, because カチカチ is never 鈍い in any sentence. Visible: something has been clamped so tight that it no longer gives — 肩ががちがち, 緊張でがちがち, がちがちの規則, ネジががちがちに締まって回らない, 守りをがちがちに固める. Who did the clamping the word does not settle: the tension may be the thing's own, and somebody else's hand may have screwed it down — がちがち rules on the result, on nothing moving any more. It is neither weight nor size: かちかち takes heavy, large things too, and がちがちに凍った雪 is fine.

Examples

  • 寒さむさで震ふるえが止とまらず、硬かたい歯はと歯はがぶつかって、離はなれた人ひとにも聞きこえる低ひくく鈍にぶい音おとでがちがちと鳴なった。He could not stop shivering in the cold; his hard teeth knocked against each other with a low, blunt sound that carried across the way.
  • その会社かいしゃの規則きそくは何年なんねんも変かわらず、例外れいがいを一ひとつも認みとめない上うえに、事情じじょうを説明せつめいしても取とり合あってもらえないほどがちがちだ。The company's rules have not changed in years, they allow not a single exception, and you can explain your circumstances all you like — nobody will hear you out.

かちゃかちゃ · to clink

Cutlery on a plate, a bunch of keys.

Voiceless か keeps the clink thin and quiet — it bothers nobody, whereas がちゃがちゃ makes that same clink insistent: you can think through かちゃかちゃ and not through がちゃがちゃ.

Examples

  • 洗あらい終おわった食器しょっきを重かさねると、皿さらとスプーンが軽かるく当あたり、高たかく細ほそい音おとが手元てもとだけでかちゃかちゃと鳴なった。Stacking the washed dishes, plate and spoon met lightly and rang out high and thin — audible only up close.
  • 軽かるい鍵束かぎたばが高たかく細ほそい音おとを手元てもとだけで立たててかちゃかちゃしている。A light bunch of keys rings out high and thin, only right there in the hand.

がちゃがちゃ · to clatter and bang

A heavy metal door, a toolbox.

Voiced が does not change the kind of sound — it is still metal on metal — it changes WHAT is clinking and how much it gets in the way: かちゃかちゃ is the thin clink of a teaspoon or a bunch of keys you can think through, がちゃがちゃ the clink of bigger, heavier things (an iron door, a toolbox), which you cannot. From its neighbours it differs by the kind of noise or by how long it lasts: がたがた is the dull rattle of something heavy shaking, not a metallic clink, and がらがら carries the emptiness of a hall rather than the contents of a toolbox. バタン and ガシャン are single events — one slam of a door, one crash of breaking glass — while がちゃがちゃ goes on for as long as somebody keeps rummaging.

Examples

  • 大おおきな金属きんぞくの扉とびらが、耳みみをふさぎたくなるほど鋭するどい音おとを響ひびかせて何度なんどもがちゃがちゃと鳴なった。The big metal door clanged sharply, again and again, until you wanted to cover your ears.
  • 大おおきな工具箱こうぐばこの中なかで金具かなぐどうしがぶつかり、鋭するどい音おとが大おおきく響ひびいてがちゃがちゃしている。Inside the big toolbox metal knocks against metal and a sharp sound rings out loudly.

からから · to be bone-dry; to clatter dry and hollow

A drained water bottle, a parched throat, a can with not a drop inside, clogs on cobbles.

からから rules on there being nothing inside — no moisture and no contents: 喉がからから is “my throat is parched”, and a shaken-out can is からから. The same word names the sound of that emptiness, because a dry, hollow thing answers exactly like that: 空き缶がからからと鳴る, 下駄がからからと鳴る, からからと笑う. That, and not a split between state and sound, is where the border with かたかた runs: かたかた is a sound too, but it settles nothing about the contents — it is a loose part knocking against something hard, and a lid clatters the same on a full pot as on an empty one. がらがら is not the stronger form either: it is about the absence of people (がらがらの電車), a different emptiness — the two are easy to confuse, but neither is the stronger version of the other.

Examples

  • 中なかをのぞいても何なにも入はいっていない軽かるい缶かんは、内側うちがわまで乾かわいてからからになった。Look inside the light can and there is nothing there; it has dried out right through.
  • 一滴いってきも残のこっていない軽かるい水筒すいとうは、すっかり乾かわいてからからだ。Not a drop is left in the light flask; it has dried out completely.

がらがら · to be empty — and to rattle hollow in that emptiness

A carriage with no passengers, a deserted hall, a heavy door carrying an echo.

がらがら is about the absence of people (座席ががらがら) and about the hollow rattle such a space carries. All it shares with からから is the kana skeleton; that word is about a dry emptiness inside a thing, not about a hall with nobody in it. がたがた is the shaking and banging of the thing itself and がちゃがちゃ the metallic clink of objects knocking together; neither says anything about the hall being empty, which is exactly what がらがら carries.

Examples

  • 座席ざせきが一ひとつも埋うまっていない大おおきな車両しゃりょうで、重おもい引ひき戸どが虚うつろに響ひびいてがらがらと開ひらいた。Not a single seat was taken in the large carriage, and the heavy sliding door rolled open with a hollow rattle that carried through the emptiness.
  • 開演前かいえんまえの大おおきなホールは席せきが半分はんぶんも埋うまらず、重おもい扉とびらの音おとだけが響ひびいてがらがらだ。Before curtain-up the big hall is not even half full; only the heavy door echoes.

かりかり · to crunch finely

Roasted beans, almonds.

Voiceless か keeps the crunch small and appetising — here hardness is a virtue. がりがり takes a bigger, harder mouthful, until it grates (ice, a bone). ぱりぱり is about something thin and brittle snapping flat (nori, crackling), where かりかり is a hard grain crunching under the tooth; さくさく crunches short and quietly, like batter or puff pastry, while かりかり is harder and plainly audible.

Examples

  • よく炒いった豆まめは小ちいさくて乾かわいて硬かたく、香こうばしくて手てが止とまらず、噛かむとかりかりと音おとがする。Well-roasted beans are small, dry and hard, so fragrant you cannot stop, and they crunch when you bite them.
  • 殻からを割わって取とり出だしたアーモンドは小ちいさくて軽かるく、乾かわいて硬かたく、歯触はざわりがかりかりだ。An almond cracked out of its shell is small and light, dry and hard, and that is how it crunches.

がりがり · to scrape or gnaw

Shaving ice, a dog on a bone.

Voiced が enlarges the mouthful and sharpens it: the same hard biting, only かりかり crunches small and appetising while がりがり grates on the ear — which is why it takes ice and bone rather than a roasted almond.

Examples

  • 大おおきな氷こおりの塊かたまりは硬かたく、削けずると粗あらい削けずりかすを残のこしてがりがりと音おとを立たてる。The big block of ice is hard; scraping it grates and leaves coarse shavings.
  • 犬いぬが大おおきくて硬かたい骨ほねを削けずるようにがりがりとかじっている。The dog gnaws at a big, hard bone as if he were shaving it down.
