ぺこぺこ · to be hungry

An empty stomach — the everyday word for hunger.

Half-voiced ぺ is light and a little pitiful. It speaks of emptiness, not pain.

Examples

  • 朝あさから何なにも食たべていないので、お腹なかがぺこぺこだ。I have not eaten a thing since morning and my stomach is completely empty.
  • お腹なかがぺこぺこの状態じょうたいで走はしると、力ちからが出でない。Running on an empty stomach, you have no strength.

くたくた · to be worn out

Limpness from wear: a body, but also a thing, that has lost its firmness.

The limpness sits in the root くた (as in くたびれる, “to tire out, to wear out”), not in the voiceless く itself. The same root with the 濁点 stands in ぐったり, but what separates it from くたくた is not the diacritic — it is the shape: AっBり shows a body that has already gone slack, plain to see from outside, and it attaches with する／と, not だ／になる. Hence くたくたのシャツ and くたくたに煮る: the same word describes a worn-out thing, and へとへと is never said of a thing at all.

Examples

  • 着古きふるしたシャツは襟えりの張はりがなくなり、洗あらっても二度にどと元もとに戻もどらず、もうくたくただ。The worn-out shirt has lost the firmness in its collar and no longer comes back to shape in the wash — it is completely worn out.
  • 鍋なべの野菜やさいを、箸はしで持もち上あげると崩くずれるほどくたくたになるまで煮込にこんだ。I simmered the vegetables in the pot until they went completely limp and fell apart when lifted with chopsticks.

ずきずき · to throb with pain

Pain that returns with your pulse.

Voiced ず is heavy and deep, but there is no pair here: neither すきすき nor ぢくぢく exists. What separates it from ちくちく is not a diacritic but a rhythm — the pain comes back in waves with the pulse instead of pricking at one point. The other two neighbours part just as plainly: がんがん pounds the whole head, heavy and dull, while じんじん does not hurt at all — it goes numb and tingles.

Examples

  • 虫歯むしばが脈打みゃくうつようにずきずきする。The bad tooth throbs in time with my pulse.
  • ぶつけた所ところが赤あかく腫はれ上あがり、しびれはないのに鼓動こどうのたびにずきずきと痛いたむ。The place I banged is red and swollen; there is no numbness, yet with every heartbeat it throbs with pain.

ちくちく · to prickle

Small repeated pain, like a needle.

Voiceless ち is fine and sharp — many small pricks, not one big one. Pricking, not itching: むずむず makes you want to scratch, ちくちく to take off whatever is pricking you. And ずきずき throbs in time with the pulse instead of stabbing at one point.

Examples

  • セーターの毛けが刺ささって、背中せなかがちくちくして、痒かゆいのではなく痛いたい。The wool of the sweater digs in and my back prickles — it does not itch, it hurts.
  • 細ほそい針はりが刺ささったように、指ゆびがちくちくと痛いたい。My finger prickles as if a fine needle had gone into it.

くらくら · to feel dizzy

The room spins.

Voiceless く is lighter: your head sways, not the furniture. くらくら names the sensation in the head itself — the room turns, everything goes dark; ふらふら names something else: that nothing is settled, neither the step nor the thought. Both can end with you crouching down, so failing to stay on your feet settles nothing here — what settles it is whether the world spins or your head fogs over. ぐるぐる, by contrast, describes a thing that really turns round, not the person looking.

Examples

  • 健康診断けんこうしんだんの採血さいけつのあと、目めの前まえが真まっ暗くらになって天井てんじょうが回まわり、その場ばにしゃがみ込こむほどくらくらした。After the blood draw at my check-up everything went black, the ceiling turned, and my head swam so badly that I crouched down where I stood.
  • サウナに長ながく入はいりすぎて、天井てんじょうが回まわるようにくらくらし、立たっていられずしゃがみ込こんだ。I stayed in the sauna too long; my head is spinning as if the ceiling were turning, and I had to crouch because I could not stay on my feet.

ぐらぐら · to wobble

An unsteady thing: a table, a tooth, a shelf.

Voiced ぐ is heavier and bigger — an object wobbles, not a feeling. What separates the neighbours is what has happened to the fixing: ぐらぐら wobbles because its mounting has given way and the thing is about to go over; がたがた knocks and rattles because there is play between the parts; ゆらゆら sways calmly, with nothing at stake.

Examples

  • ねじが緩ゆるんだ眼鏡めがねのつるが、音おともなく片側かたがわへ傾かたむいて揺ゆれ、ぐらぐらして掛かけていられない。A screw in my glasses has worked loose: the temple tips silently to one side and wobbles too much to wear them.
  • 抜ぬけかけの乳歯にゅうしが、指ゆびで押おすと片側かたがわへ傾かたむき、落おち着つかないほどぐらぐらと動うごく。The loose milk tooth already gives: press it with a finger and it tips to one side, wobbling too much to ignore.

ひりひり · to sting, to burn

A burning sting on skin or tongue.

Voiceless ひ is delicate — the surface burns, not the inside.

Examples

  • 日ひに焼やけた肩かたにシャワーの湯ゆがしみるほど、焼やけるようにひりひりする。My sunburnt shoulders sting as if they were on fire; even the shower water smarts.
  • 辛からい物ものを食たべて、舌したが焼やけるようにひりひりする。I ate something spicy and my tongue is burning.

びりびり · to tingle sharply

Electricity through the body; also: tearing.

Voiced び is strong and sudden — it does not burn, it jolts. What separates it from じんじん is time: びりびり passes through like a discharge and is gone, while じんじん stays long after the cause has stopped.

Examples

  • 肘ひじをぶつけて、電気でんきが走はしったように腕うでがびりびりした。I banged my elbow and a jolt like electricity shot through my arm.
  • ドアノブに触ふれた瞬間しゅんかん、指先ゆびさきにびりびりと静電気せいでんきが走はしった。The moment I touched the doorknob, static electricity shot through my fingertips.

がんがん · to pound

A heavy, dull headache; also noise hammering your ears.

There is a pair here, and a textbook one: かんかん is glare and anger seen from outside (かんかん照り, かんかんに怒る). The 濁点 in が lets that same force inside the head — a pounding you cannot turn down. What separates it from ずきずき is reach: がんがん pounds the whole head, heavy and dull, while ずきずき throbs at one point and in time with the pulse.

Examples

  • 二日酔ふつかよいで、鈍にぶく重おもい痛いたみが奥おくから突つき上あげ、頭あたまががんがんする。I am hungover: a dull, heavy pain pushes up from inside and my head is pounding.
  • 騒音そうおんのせいで、頭あたまの芯しんに鉛なまりが入はいったように鈍にぶく、こめかみの奥おくががんがんと痛いたむ。The noise leaves my head dull, as if lead had been poured into it, and deep in my temples it is pounding.

ぽかぽか · to be pleasantly warm

Warmth spreading through the whole body: spring sun, a hot spring, walking yourself warm.

The 清音／半濁音 pair is real here: ほかほか and ぽかぽか. Voiceless ほ keeps the warmth attached to something freshly heated — rice straight from the pot, a bun out of the steamer, someone straight out of the bath. The 半濁点 in ぽ cuts it loose from any source: the warmth arises by itself, from the sun and the weather, with nothing wrapped around you — Japanese says ぽかぽか陽気 for a mild sunny day, never ほかほか陽気. Warmth you have to wrap yourself in is ぬくぬく.

Examples

  • 春はるの日差ひざしで、体からだが芯しんからぽかぽかする。The spring sunshine leaves me pleasantly warm right through.
  • 風かぜのない小春日和こはるびよりで、日ひなたに座すわっているだけで体からだじゅうがぽかぽかだ。A windless, mild late-autumn day: just sitting in the sun leaves my whole body pleasantly warm.

むかむか · to feel nauseous

Your stomach rises; also: simmering anger.

む carries no 濁点 and still sounds muffled and heavy — the nausea wells up inside, without sharp pain. The nasal m is itself voiced: 清音 means no diacritic, not no voice.

Examples

  • 船ふねに酔よって、吐はき気けで胃いがむかむかする。I got seasick and my stomach is churning.
  • 脂あぶらっこい物ものを食たべすぎて、吐はき気けが胃いの底そこからむかむかとこみ上あげる。I ate too much greasy food and nausea keeps rising from the pit of my stomach.

むずむず · to itch

An itch that will not let you sit still.

Voiced ず adds persistence: it does not itch once, it keeps itching. An itch, not a pain: ちくちく pricks and hurts, むずむず only itches and will not let you settle. The same restlessness on the pleasant side is うずうず — nothing itches there, you simply cannot wait to get on with something.

Examples

  • 花粉症かふんしょうで、痒かゆくて我慢がまんできないほど鼻はながむずむずする。My hay fever makes my nose itch past all bearing.
  • 虫むしに刺さされた所ところが痒かゆくて、かきむしりたくなるほどむずむずとして落おち着つかない。The insect bite itches so badly I want to scratch it raw; I cannot sit still.

きりきり · to have a gripping pain

A stomach that feels squeezed and twisted.

Voiceless き keeps the pain thin and sharp. The voiced ぎりぎり means something else entirely — “just barely” — so there is no pair here. What separates it from しくしく is force and pace: きりきり grips suddenly and sharply enough to catch your breath, while しくしく aches faintly, dully and without end.

Examples

  • 発表はっぴょうの前まえになると、胃いが急きゅうに締しめつけられるようにきりきりと痛いたむ。Before a presentation my stomach suddenly cramps with a sharp pain.
  • 冷つめたい物ものを飲のむと、胃いが急きゅうに締しめつけられるようにきりきりして痛いたい。When I drink something cold my stomach suddenly twists with pain.

しくしく · to ache dully and long

A weak belly ache that will not end.

The voiced じくじく does exist, but it speaks of a wound that weeps — the 濁点 shifts nothing about the pain here. The neighbour in the same belly settles it: きりきり grips suddenly and sharply, しくしく aches faintly and without end. The other neighbour lies across the line into weeping: めそめそ is snivelling and self-pity, plainly heard and seen on a person, while the しくしく of this card hurts inside and shows nothing.

Examples

  • 鈍にぶくて軽かるい痛いたみを顔かおには出ださず、朝あさからずっとお腹なかがしくしくしている。I let nothing show of the dull, mild pain, and my belly has been aching since morning.
  • お腹なかが鈍にぶくしくしくと痛いたむが、薬くすりを飲のむほどではないので表おもてに出ださないままでいる。My belly keeps aching dully, though not enough to take anything for it, so I let nothing show.

ぶるぶる · to tremble in small shivers

Shaking from cold or from fear.

The 濁点 in ぶ makes the trembling involuntary — from cold or from fear, and impossible to stop. Plain ふるふる is light and faint: it is how you shake your head to say no (ふるふると首を振る), not how you shiver in the cold. The four neighbours each leave along a different axis: がくがく shakes coarsely, at the whole joint, until the knees give, while ぶるぶる trembles finely just under the skin; ぞくぞく does not tremble at all, it runs down the back as a chill; びくびく stays inside, being a person's fear rather than a movement of the body; ぷるぷる quivers softly and pleasantly, and is said of a thing, not of someone cold.

Examples

  • 上着うわぎを忘わすれ、歯はの根ねが合あわないほどの寒さむさで、はたから見みても分わかるくらい体からだが小刻こきざみにぶるぶると震ふるえる。I forgot my jacket and the cold sets my teeth chattering; my whole body shivers in small tremors plain enough for anyone to see.
  • 恐怖きょうふで歯はの根ねが合あわず、震ふるえが指先ゆびさきまで及およんで、はたから見みても分わかるくらい小刻こきざみにぶるぶるしている。Fear sets my teeth chattering; the tremor reaches my fingertips and anyone can see it.

がくがく · to shake at the joints

Knees that shake and give way.

The amplitude sits in が, not in the 濁点 as such — ぶるぶる carries one too and still shakes finely. Open が lets the whole joint go; closed ぶ keeps the shaking at the skin. がたがた shakes just as coarsely, but a thing — a window, a cart, a rickety table — and it rattles while it does; がくがく is about the joints of a living body.

Examples

  • 長ながい下くだり坂ざかを歩あるいたあと、膝ひざが笑わらうほど大おおきくがくがくと震ふるえた。After the long walk down the slope my knees shook so hard they almost gave way.
  • 面接めんせつの順番じゅんばんを待まつうちに、膝ひざが言いうことを聞きかず、大おおきくがくがくしてきた。As I waited my turn for the interview, my knees stopped obeying me and my legs began to shake.

へとへと · to be utterly drained

Exhaustion at the very end of your strength: still on your feet, but only just.

The mark exists, the pair does not: the 濁点 gives べとべと, but that is a separate word — about stickiness, not tiredness. Here the exhaustion is carried by the repeated ABAB shape itself. What is restricted is the attachment: だ and になる, never する — 彼はへとへとだった is correct, 彼はへとへとする is not. Its neighbour ぐったり speaks of a body that has already gone slack (力なく横たわる, うなだれる); as long as you are still on your feet and moving, it is へとへと, not ぐったり.

Examples

  • 引ひっ越こしを手伝てつだって疲つかれ、ガス欠けつになったようにエネルギーが空からっぽなのに、足取あしどりは乱みだれず、まだ立たったまま荷にほどきを続つづけていて、もうへとへとだ。I helped with the move and I am so tired my energy has run down to zero like a tank out of fuel, and even so my step does not waver and I am still on my feet unpacking — utterly drained.
  • 子こどもを一日中いちにちじゅう追おいかけて疲つかれ、気力きりょくが底そこをついたのに足元あしもとがふらつくことはなく、座すわる間まもなく動うごき続つづけて、へとへとになった。I chased after the child all day; my last drop of energy was gone and still my legs never wavered — there was no moment to sit down and I kept moving, until I was utterly drained.

じんじん · to tingle and throb

Numbness that pulses and burns.

The 濁点 in じ makes the sensation deep and diffuse. Plain しんしん drifted off to weather and silence. What separates it from びりびり is time: びりびり jolts once and is gone, while じんじん stays long after the cause. What separates it from ずきずき is the kind of sensation: ずきずき is pain itself, じんじん is numbness that happens to pulse.

Examples

  • 正座せいざを崩くずしたあと、痛いたいほどのしびれで足あしが脈打みゃくうつようにじんじんと痛いたむ。When I came out of the formal kneeling position my legs went numb and now they throb painfully.
  • 氷水こおりみずから出だした指先ゆびさきが、痛いたいほどのしびれで脈打みゃくうつようにじんじんと熱ねつを持もった。My fingertips, taken out of the ice water, went numb to the point of pain and began to throb with heat.

ぞくぞく · to shiver down the spine

A chill down the back: fever or horror.

There is no plain そくそく in this sense, so the 濁点 settles nothing here — and neither does the shiver itself: ぞくぞく can be pleasant (楽しみでぞくぞくする). This card teaches the other one, from cold and from horror. A chill runs, it does not shake: ぶるぶる is a visible trembling of the body, while ぞくぞく goes down the back and need not show on you at all. ぞっと passes through once and is over at once; ぞくぞく keeps coming back in waves.

Examples

  • 風邪かぜの引ひき始はじめか、さっきから何度なんども寒気さむけが背筋せすじを走はしってぞくぞくする。I think I am coming down with a cold; for a while now chills have been running down my spine again and again.
  • 怪談かいだんを聞きいてから、思おもい出だすたびに、うなじの毛けが逆立さかだつような寒気さむけがぞくぞくと背筋せすじを走はしる。Ever since I heard that ghost story, every time it comes back to me a chill runs down my spine and makes the hair on the back of my neck stand up.

うとうと · to doze off

Sleep you keep falling out of.

The ABAB shape means “over and over” here — dozing off and waking. The AっBり shape (ぐっすり) would mean sleep slept through. すやすや speaks of even, quiet breathing, that is of sleep that lasts; うとうと of sleep that keeps breaking.

Examples

  • 電車でんしゃの揺ゆれが心地ここちよく、ゆっくり眠気ねむけが来きて、夢ゆめうつつのままうとうとする。The sway of the train is so pleasant that sleep creeps up on me and I drift off half awake.
  • 暖あたたかい日差ひざしの中なか、気持きもちのいい眠気ねむけにゆっくり負まけ、何度なんども目めを覚さましては夢ゆめうつつのままうとうとと船ふねをこいだ。In the warm sunshine I slowly gave in to a pleasant drowsiness and kept nodding off and waking again.

すやすや · to sleep peacefully

The even, quiet breathing of a sleeper.

Voiceless す keeps the breathing even and quiet. What separates its neighbours is not a diacritic but the sleep itself: うとうと keeps breaking, ぐっすり is deep and slept right through, and すやすや says nothing about depth — which is why it fits a light sleep too, as long as the breathing stays even. The word itself lives on a verb — すやすや眠る, never すやすやする.

Examples

  • 抱だき上あげればすぐ目めを覚さますほど眠ねむりは浅あさいのに、赤あかちゃんは規則正きそくただしい寝息ねいきを立たててすやすやと眠ねむっている。The baby sleeps so lightly it would wake the moment I picked it up, and yet it breathes evenly and sleeps peacefully.
  • 泣なき疲つかれた子こどもは、声こえをかければすぐ目めを覚さますほど浅あさい眠ねむりのまま、寝息ねいきの間隔かんかくが乱みだれずすやすやと眠ねむっていた。The child had cried itself to sleep and slept so lightly a single word would have woken it, yet its breathing kept an even rhythm and it slept peacefully.

よろよろ · to totter along

No strength left in the legs, and every step is close to a fall.

There is no voiced partner here. The dictionary describes this word in a single line — 力なく倒れそうになりながら歩くさま — and that is all of it: walking with a body that has run out of strength. Nothing about a destination; 酔ってよろよろと歩き回る is an ordinary sentence. So よろよろ is simply narrower than ふらふら: ふらふら takes the same scene and adds dizziness (熱でふらふらする) and aimless wandering (目的もなくふらふらする), where よろよろ no longer fits.

Examples

  • 疲つかれで足あしに力ちからが残のこっておらず、今いまにも倒たおれそうなのに、一度いちども休やすまずにゆっくりよろよろと歩あるいて帰かえった。Tiredness left no strength at all in my legs and I was about to go down at any moment, and still I did not rest even once, tottering slowly all the way home.
  • 荷物にもつの重おもみが肩かたに食くい込こんで足あしが疲つかれきり、一歩いっぽずつしか進すすめず、それでも立たち止どまらないままよろよろする。The weight of the pack digs into my shoulder, my legs are worn out and I move one step at a time — and still I do not stop, tottering along.

ちかちか · to sting, of the eyes

Fine, flickering light; this card takes the use about the eyes it hurts.

Voiceless ち makes the stimulus fine and pinpoint. The dictionary gives two uses and both are ordinary: the small light blinking on and off (電気がちかちかする, 星がちかちか光る), and the eyes that this glare hurts (目がちかちかする). This card teaches the second one — hence the sentences about the person looking. The separate word ぴかぴか speaks of a steady, strong shine; it is no voicing pair, because 半濁点 belongs to the は row: the plain counterpart of ぴか is ひか (from 光る), not ちか. What separates them is the root, not the diacritic. ちらちら flickers too, but it describes the thing that flickers — snow, a light, something caught out of the corner of the eye; the ちかちか of this card is about the eyes that the glare hurts.

Examples

  • 暗くらい部屋へやでスマホをつけたら、細こまかい光ひかりが明滅めいめつを繰くり返かえして、見みているこちらの目めがちかちかとして痛いたく、思おもわず閉とじたくなった。I turned on my phone in a dark room: the fine light flickered on and off again and again, and from looking at it my eyes stung so much I wanted to shut them without thinking.
  • 雪ゆきの照てり返かえしがまぶしく、細こまかい光ひかりが次つぎから次つぎへと目めの奥おくで瞬またたいて、見みているこちらの目めがちかちかして、涙なみだが出でるほど痛いたい。The glare off the snow dazzles, fine light flickers one after another deep in the eyes, and from looking at it my eyes sting until they water.

かっか · to burn with heat

A face or head on fire: shame, anger, fever.

The small っ inside gives a burst of heat that the repeated ぽかぽか lacks — there the warmth is mild and pleasant.

Examples

  • 恥はずかしさで耳みみまで赤あかくなり、顔かおが焼やけるように熱あつくなってかっかする。Shame turned me red to the ears and my face is burning hot.
  • 言いい返かえせなかった悔くやしさで顔かおのほてりが引ひかず、焼やけるように熱あつく、まだかっかしている。The frustration of having had no answer keeps the flush on my face; it is still burning hot.

ひくひく · to twitch faintly

An eyelid or the corner of a mouth twitching on its own.

Voiceless ひ keeps the movement microscopic and neutral. The 濁点 form びくびく would turn a muscle twitch into a person's fear.

Examples

  • 寝不足ねぶそくのせいか、まぶたが同おなじリズムで小ちいさく震ふるえてひくひくする。From lack of sleep, perhaps, my eyelid keeps twitching faintly in the same rhythm.
  • 笑わらいをこらえて、口くちの端はしが同おなじリズムで小ちいさくひくひくと震ふるえた。As I held back a laugh, the corner of my mouth twitched faintly in the same rhythm.

ぬくぬく · to be snug and warm

Warmth that wraps around you: a quilt, a thick coat.

Enveloping warmth rather than radiating warmth. ぽかぽか arises by itself, from the sun and the weather, with nothing wrapped around you (ぽかぽか陽気). ほかほか is the warmth of something freshly heated: rice straight from the pot, a bun out of the steamer. ぬくぬく is warmth you have to wrap yourself in, and it speaks of the person who is snug in it.

Examples

  • 湯ゆたんぽを入いれた柔やわらかい布団ふとんに肩かたまですっぽり包つつまれて、朝あさまでぬくぬくと温あたたかい。I slipped a hot-water bottle into the soft bedding, and wrapped in it up to my shoulders I stay snug and warm until morning.
  • 厚あつくて柔やわらかいダウンジャケットが体からだを包つつみ込こむように温あたたかく、雪ゆきの日ひでも着きている人ひとはぬくぬくしていられる。A thick, soft down jacket wraps right around the body, so whoever wears it stays snug and warm even on a snowy day.

ぶよぶよ · to be flabby and soft

A body that has lost its firmness.

The 濁点 makes the softness unhealthy and heavy. Light ふわふわ is not its voiceless counterpart — that would be ふよふよ; ふわふわ differs in its second mora, not in a diacritic.

Examples

  • 運動不足うんどうぶそくで、お腹なかの肉にくがつまめるほど重おもく柔やわらかくなってぶよぶよだ。For want of exercise my belly has gone heavy and soft enough to take hold of.
  • 久ひさしぶりに鏡かがみを見みたら、締しまりのない、重おもくて柔やわらかいぶよぶよの体からだになっていた。I looked in the mirror after a long time: my body had gone slack, heavy and flabby.

ぱんぱん · to be swollen tight

Legs or a belly so full they might burst.

The 半濁点 gives fullness stretched to its limit. The 濁点 form ばんばん would be banging, not a state.

Examples

  • 観光かんこうで一日中いちにちじゅう歩あるいた疲つかれで、ふくらはぎがはち切きれそうに張はってぱんぱんだ。After a whole day of sightseeing my calves are stretched so tight they could burst.
  • 立たちっぱなしの仕事しごとで、夕方ゆうがたには疲つかれた足あしがはち切きれそうにぱんぱんになる。Because I am on my feet all day at work, by evening my tired legs are swollen tight.
